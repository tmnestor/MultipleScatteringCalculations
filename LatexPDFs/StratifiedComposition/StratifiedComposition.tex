\title{\bfseries Composing the Stratified and Lateral Propagators\\[4pt]
\large Reciprocity Gates, the Source-Convention Reconciliation,\\
and the Case for a Depth-Stratified Crust Reference}
\author{}
\date{26 July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The thesis formulates heterogeneous ocean crust as a \emph{two-potential}
(distorted-wave) problem: a laterally invariant, depth-dependent reference
solved exactly, plus a lateral contrast summed as a multiple-scattering series
\emph{carried by the reference Green's tensor}.  Realising this numerically
requires composing a stratified vertical propagator $\Pz$ with a
disorder-resolved lateral propagator $\Px$ in a single resolvent.  This note
records what had to be established before that composition was legitimate.
Four results: (i) the two-stage solve is \emph{algebraically identical} to one
global Foldy--Lax solve with $\Px+\Pz$; (ii) the crust gradient must live in
the reference, not in the scattering series --- a $\pm0.5\%$ velocity gradient
changes the inter-scatterer coupling by $10\%$; (iii) the two available
propagators use \emph{different source representations}, a state jump versus a
body force, related by one factor of the system matrix; and (iv) with that
conversion and an adjoint source operator, both satisfy a common reciprocity
invariant to machine precision.  Six errata in the thesis were found and
corrected along the way.
\end{abstract}

\section{The object being built}

The thesis splits the medium into a laterally invariant, depth-dependent
reference --- the depth average, which generally increases with depth --- plus
a lateral contrast superimposed as cylinders.  The first potential is solved
exactly by Kennett-like matrix recursions; the second is summed as a
multiple-scattering series.  The master result of Chapter~5 is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LSstrat}
  \mathcal{G} = {}_{r}\mathcal{G}
  + \mathcal{P}^{z,R}_{s}\,\dC\,
    \Bigl(\bI - \bigl[\Pz + \Px\bigr]\dC\Bigr)^{-1}
    {}_{r}\mathcal{G}^{z}_{r},
\end{equation}
with $\Qb \equiv (\bI - \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal{E})^{-1}
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{int}}$ the extension of the plane-wave propagator to a
stratified medium, and $\Pz \equiv -i\,\Theta^{z}_{\mathrm{in}}\Qb
\Theta^{z}_{\mathrm{ou}}$.  Because $\Qb$ depends only on the reference, it is
built once per frequency and every Krylov iterate is then a cheap sweep.  That
economy is the point of the splitting.

\subsection{The two-stage solve is exact}

The thesis first solves each row internally,
$\dT = \dC(\bI - \Px\dC)^{-1}$, and then couples rows through $\Pz$.  The
collapse to~\eqref{eq:LSstrat} is a resolvent identity: with
$A(\bI-PA)^{-1} = (A^{-1}-P)^{-1}$ and $\dT^{-1} = \dC^{-1} - \Px$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
  \dT\bigl(\bI - \Pz\dT\bigr)^{-1}
  \;=\;\bigl(\dC^{-1} - \Px - \Pz\bigr)^{-1}
  \;=\;\dC\Bigl(\bI - \bigl[\Px+\Pz\bigr]\dC\Bigr)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Solving rows first and then stacking them is algebraically identical to
one global Foldy--Lax solve with $P = \Px + \Pz$.}  The row/stack division is a
computational strategy, not different physics.  Verified numerically at
$n = 1,3,9,27$ to $3\times10^{-16}$, and independently against the global
Neumann series $\sum_{k}\dC(P\dC)^{k}$ to $4.7\times10^{-16}$.

This is what licenses building $\Px$ and $\Pz$ separately and adding them.

\section{The crust gradient belongs in the reference}

An earlier design made the crust reference a single homogeneous block, with the
internal crust interfaces transparent, purely to reduce cost.  That choice
moves the depth gradient out of the reference and into the scattering series.
Its size is measurable.

Holding the geometry fixed --- same ocean, seabed, half-space and interface
depths --- and varying only whether the crust reference carries its gradient,
the relative change in the inter-scatterer coupling $G_{9}(i\!\leftarrow\!j)$ is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
crust $\alpha$ gradient & complex difference & magnitude only \\
\midrule
$4.00$--$4.00$ (uniform)          & $0.0\%$  & $0.0\%$ \\
$3.98$--$4.02$ ($\pm0.5\%$)       & $\mathbf{10.5\%}$ & $8.4\%$ \\
$3.95$--$4.05$                    & $26.5\%$ & $23.2\%$ \\
$3.90$--$4.10$                    & $55.6\%$ & $50.3\%$ \\
$3.00$--$5.00$ (realistic, $67\%$)& ${\sim}130\%$ & ${\sim}35\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Controls: the null test (identical models) returns exactly zero, and the
difference grows smoothly from zero with gradient strength, so this is physics
rather than a coding artefact.  Across $f = 5,10,20$~Hz and horizontal slowness
$p = 0$--$0.2$~s/km the realistic case runs $70$--$340\%$ uniformly: the
operator is \emph{different}, not perturbed.

A $\pm0.5\%$ velocity gradient across the crust changes the coupling by $10\%$
because the effect is a travel-time accumulation along the path --- the
``habitual mistiming'' the thesis quotes Coates \& Charrette on, and precisely
what the two-potential split exists to remove.  \textbf{The uniform-host crust
is not a small correction and should not be used as a cost saving.}

\section{The source-convention obstruction}

Two 9-component propagators are available.  Both claim the state
$(u_z,u_x,u_y,\ \varepsilon_{zz},\varepsilon_{xx},\varepsilon_{yy},\
2\varepsilon_{xy},2\varepsilon_{zy},2\varepsilon_{zx})$, but they are built
from opposite ends, and the difference is \emph{not} a normalisation.

\subsection{A calibrated invariant}

Reciprocity is the right discriminator: it holds in any elastic medium, so no
uniform limit or $k\leftrightarrow r$ transform is needed, and it is sensitive
to exactly the source-side weighting at issue.

For the validated closed-form propagator $P = [[G,C],[H,S]]$ the elementwise
ratio $P_{ab}/P_{ba}$ is an \emph{exact} outer product $w_a/w_b$ with
$w = (1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2)$ --- the Voigt engineering factor, strain rows
carrying $2\varepsilon$ on the shears while stress columns carry plain
$\sigma$.  Hence the invariant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Winv}
  \bW P \ \text{is symmetric},\qquad
  \bW = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(1,1,1,\ 1,1,1,\ \tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\bigr),
\end{equation}
satisfied to $4.2\times10^{-18}$.  This was \emph{measured, not assumed}: the
first version of the check asserted plain symmetry and failed at $10$--$16\%$.
A gate whose calibration leg is not itself checked is worthless.

\subsection{The layered propagator obeys a different law}

The layered $9\times9$ fails~\eqref{eq:Winv} at ${\sim}110\%$, and the failure
survives every diagonal rescaling.  Dropping to the $6\times6$ core, its exact
law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gateD}
  M(i\!\leftarrow\! j)(+k)
  = \frac{i}{\omega}\,\bS\bD\,
    \bJ_6 \bigl[M(j\!\leftarrow\! i)(-k)\bigr]^{T}\!\bJ_6\,\bS\bD,
  \qquad
  \bS\bD = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(1,-1,-1,\,-i\omega,\,+i\omega,\,+i\omega\bigr),
\end{equation}
verified to $6\times10^{-16}$.  It factors into
$\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-i\omega,-i\omega,-i\omega)$ --- the
$\sigma/(-i\omega)$ mixed basis, applied uniformly to P--SV and SH --- times
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,1,-1,-1)$, the parity of the $x,y$ components
under $k\to-k$.  The symplectic signature is required: the joint rank-one fit
residual is $4.8\times10^{-15}$ with $\bJ_6$ and $0.97$ with the identity.

\paragraph{A trap.}  Recovering the SH entries \emph{requires} $k_y\neq0$.  At
$k_y=0$ the SH channel decouples, components $2$ and $5$ vanish identically,
and a least-squares fit returns an unconstrained value for them --- in our case
a plausible-looking $\sqrt{\omega}$ that is simply wrong.

\subsection{Translation invariance clears the solver}

Before treating this as a convention issue it had to be ruled out as a defect,
since a genuinely non-reciprocal Green's function would invalidate everything
built on it.  In a homogeneous medium $G(i\!\leftarrow\! j)$ must depend only
on $z_i - z_j$, and \emph{a constant basis weight cannot break that} --- the
same weights appear at every interface and cancel.  The test therefore probes
the Green's function alone.  Two independent knobs, both monotone to zero with
no plateau: attenuation $Q = 3\to0.5$ gives $6.0\times10^{-2}\to
5.6\times10^{-4}$; moving the test pairs deeper at fixed $Q$ gives
$2.5\times10^{-1}\to1.7\times10^{-5}$.  The residual is free-surface and seabed
leakage behaving exactly as leakage must.  \textbf{The layered solver is
sound.}

\section{The conversion: state jump versus body force}

The obstruction is structural.  The layered Green's function injects a unit
\emph{jump in the displacement--traction state}; the closed-form propagator
takes a \emph{(force, stress-glut)} source.  These are different objects, and
no rescaling converts one into the other.

Integrating $\partial_z b = \bA b + F_1\delta(z-z_S) + F_2\delta'(z-z_S)$
across $z_S$: a $\delta'$ term produces a state jump $[b] = F_2$, a $\delta$
term produces none.  With the Box~5.3 projection
$\Sigma = -i\,\bD_z(-k)^{T}\bJ_6\,(F_1 + \bA F_2)$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conversion}
  \boxed{\;\text{state jump } S \;\equiv\; \text{body force } F = \bA\,S\;}
\end{equation}
where $\bA$ is the Fourier-domain system matrix.  Since
$\lVert\operatorname{offdiag}\bA\rVert = 5.6\times10^{6}$, the conversion
genuinely \emph{mixes} components --- which is why no diagonal weight could
ever have reconciled the two conventions.  Derivation and the earlier numerical
experiment agree.

Validated headless in Mathematica against the thesis's own identities: the
quasi-Hamiltonian relation $\bA^{T}(-k)\bJ_6 + \bJ_6\bA(k) = 0$ to $0$;
eigenvalues $= i\operatorname{diag}(\pm k_z)$ to $0$; $\bA\bD_z = \bD_z\Lambda$
against the energy-normalised eigenbasis to $7\times10^{-12}$; and Box~5.3's
own worked point-explosion example reproduced exactly, both the intermediate
$F_1 + \bA F_2$ and the closed-form $\Sigma \propto (1,0,0,1,0,0)$ --- P only,
equal up and down, as an explosion must radiate.

\section{The reconciliation}

Two corrections suffice, and both are forced rather than fitted.

\paragraph{(1) Source normalisation.}  Requiring the corrected $6\times6$ to
obey clean symplectic reciprocity $N_1 = \bJ_6 N_2^{T}\bJ_6$, and matching
against~\eqref{eq:gateD} using
$\bJ_6\operatorname{diag}(d_A,d_B)\bJ_6 = -\operatorname{diag}(d_B,d_A)$,
determines the source-side factor uniquely up to the receiver-side freedom.
Taking the receiver side as the identity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
  N = M\,\bQ,\qquad
  \bQ = \operatorname{diag}\Bigl(1,-1,-1,\ \tfrac{i}{\omega},
        -\tfrac{i}{\omega},-\tfrac{i}{\omega}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
after which the $6\times6$ obeys clean symplectic reciprocity to
$6.5\times10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{(2) Adjoint source operator.}  The existing source operator is plain
Hooke's law and wavenumber-independent, while the receiver operator $A(k)$ is
not.  Requiring $A_1N_1B_1 = \bW^{-1}(A_2N_2B_2)^{T}\bW$ given
$N_1 = \bJ_6N_2^{T}\bJ_6$ forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
  B(k) = -\,\bJ_6\,A_{\mathrm{src}}(-k)^{T}\,\bW,
\end{equation}
built with the \emph{source} interface's material.  Using the receiver's
material instead leaves an $83\%$ residual --- the kind of error that a
structural test would never catch.

With~\eqref{eq:Q} and~\eqref{eq:B}, the corrected $9\times9$ satisfies the
closed-form invariant~\eqref{eq:Winv} to $9.7\times10^{-16}$ across
$f = 5,10,25$~Hz and three interface pairs.  The conventions are reconciled and
the composition~\eqref{eq:LSstrat} is unblocked.

\subsection{Why the existing tests could not have found this}

Every test in the layered suite is structural: shape, finiteness,
zero-$T$-no-perturbation, Born error quadratic in $\lVert T\rVert$,
single-layer Foldy equals Born.  \emph{All of them pass for any self-consistent
source convention}, because each is defined relative to that convention.  An
invariant --- reciprocity, energy balance, a closed-form limit --- is what
discriminates.  This is a recurring pattern in the project and the reason the
gates recorded here are worth keeping in continuous integration rather than
run on request.

others would be caught by a careful reader, whereas the symbol collision
produces a wrong matrix with nothing in the text to flag it.

\midrule

Its reciprocity law                & known      & $6\times10^{-16}$ \\
Jump $\leftrightarrow$ force       & derived    & Box 5.3, validated \\
Corrected $9\times9$               & reconciled & $9.7\times10^{-16}$ \\

\midrule

What remains is implementation rather than investigation: assemble
\[
  \Bigl(\bI - \bigl[\Pz + \Px\bigr]\dC\Bigr)\psi
\]
with $\Pz$ from the corrected layered propagator and $\Px$ from the existing
per-voxel FFT kernel, and drive it with the existing Krylov solver.  The
lateral operator already handles disorder --- its block-Toeplitz structure
comes from the regular \emph{mesh}, not from the scatterers being identical ---
so no new operator is required.

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Composing the stratified vertical propagator with the disorder-resolved lateral
propagator in a single resolvent requires both to speak the same
$9$-component language.  They did not.  This note defines every object
involved, states every test applied to them and its exact outcome, and then
resolves the discrepancy: three defects, two of them inside the stratified
$6\times6$ and one a sign in the source operator.  GATE~F goes from
$0.40$--$0.81$ to $1.1\times10^{-15}$ and GATE~D to $5\times10^{-16}$ with no
weight at all.  The source operator, long suspected, was never the defect: it
follows in closed form from the governing equation and agrees with the thesis's
own worked example to $0.000\times10^{0}$.  The correction carries over to
stratified media unchanged.  GATE~E turns out to be a \emph{false} gate --- the
exact reference fails it too, and it was passing because of a defect --- which
accounts for negative result N1 entirely.  What remains is to lattice-sum
$\bP^{z}$ into the discrete representation, a construction rather than a search.
It is written to be recoverable without reference to any prior discussion:
every symbol is defined where it first appears, every numerical claim names the
script that produced it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conventions and objects}

\subsection{Indices and ordering}

Cartesian indices use the seismological convention of this project:
\begin{equation}
  z = 0 \ (\text{down}), \qquad x = 1 \ (\text{right}), \qquad y = 2 \ (\text{out}),
\end{equation}
right-handed.  Voigt pairs are ordered
\begin{equation}\label{eq:voigt}
  \alpha = 1,\dots,6 \ \longleftrightarrow\ (p,q) =
  (0,0),\ (1,1),\ (2,2),\ (1,2),\ (0,2),\ (0,1),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $(zz,\ xx,\ yy,\ xy,\ zy,\ zx)$.  Shear components carry the
\emph{engineering} factor: the strain vector holds $2\varepsilon$ on the last
three slots, while the stress vector holds plain $\sigma$.  This asymmetry is
the origin of the weight $\bW$ below and is not optional --- it is how the
existing code is written.

Time convention is $e^{-i\omega t}$ throughout.  Units are seismic: km/s,
g/cm$^3$, GPa, km.

\subsection{The two state vectors}

The \emph{displacement--traction $6$-vector} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b6}
  \mathbf{b} \;=\; \bigl(u_z,\ u_x,\ u_y,\ \tau_{zz},\ \tau_{xz},\ \tau_{yz}\bigr)^{T},
\end{equation}
the natural state for a layered medium, since these six quantities are
continuous across horizontal interfaces.

The \emph{displacement--strain $9$-vector} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9}
  \boldsymbol{\psi} \;=\;
  \bigl(u_z,\ u_x,\ u_y,\
        \varepsilon_{zz},\ \varepsilon_{xx},\ \varepsilon_{yy},\
        2\varepsilon_{xy},\ 2\varepsilon_{zy},\ 2\varepsilon_{zx}\bigr)^{T},
\end{equation}
the natural state for a scatterer, since the single-site $T$-matrix acts on
displacement and strain.

\subsection{The two weights}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
  \bW \;=\; \operatorname{diag}\bigl(1,1,1,\ 1,1,1,\ \tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\bigr)
  \qquad (9\times9),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J6}
  \bJ_6 \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{0}_3 & \bI_3 \\ -\bI_3 & \mathbf{0}_3 \end{pmatrix}
  \qquad (6\times6),
\end{equation}
the canonical symplectic array of the thesis (its Eq.~\textsc{Jdef}).  Note
$\bJ_6^{T} = -\bJ_6$ and $\bJ_6^2 = -\bI_6$.

\section{The three propagators}

\subsection{$\bP^{\text{cf}}$ --- the closed-form whole-space propagator}

\texttt{cubic\_scattering.horizontal\_greens.exact\_propagator\_9x9}$(x,y,z,\omega,\text{ref})$
returns the $9\times9$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pcf}
  \bP^{\text{cf}}(\mathbf{r}) =
  \begin{pmatrix} G & C \\ H & S \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
built from the Kupradze whole-space Green's tensor and its derivatives, where
$G$ is $3\times3$ (force $\to$ displacement), $C$ is $3\times6$ (Voigt stress
source $\to$ displacement), $H$ is $6\times3$ (force $\to$ Voigt strain), and
$S$ is $6\times6$ (Voigt stress source $\to$ Voigt strain).  Its \emph{source}
is a pair $(F_i,\ \Delta\sigma^{*}_{V\alpha})$ --- a body force and a
stress-glut --- and its \emph{output} is $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ of~\eqref{eq:v9}.
This object is treated here as ground truth; \S\ref{sec:gateA} states the
evidence.

\subsection{$\bG_6$ --- the layered displacement--traction Green's function}

\texttt{GlobalMatrix.layered\_greens.layered\_greens\_6x6}$(\text{model},\omega,k_x,k_y,j,i)$
returns a $6\times6$ mapping a source at interface $j$ to the state
$\mathbf{b}$ at interface $i$, at horizontal wavenumber $(k_x,k_y)$, for a
plane-layered model.  Two properties matter and are easy to miss:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Its \textbf{source is a state jump}, not a body force.  Internally
      (\texttt{riccati\_greens\_psv}) a unit discontinuity is injected into the
      state vector, \texttt{rhs\_p += I4}.
\item Its \textbf{basis is mixed}.  The docstring of
      \texttt{riccati\_greens\_psv} gives it as
      $[u_x,\ u_z,\ \sigma_{zz}/(-i\omega),\ \sigma_{xz}/(-i\omega)]$: the
      traction half is scaled by $1/(-i\omega)$ relative to physical traction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{$\Asys$ --- the Fourier-domain system matrix}

The layered problem marches as $\partial_z \mathbf{b} = \Asys \mathbf{b}$ with
(thesis Eq.~\textsc{Akdef})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Asys}
  \Asys(k_x,k_y) =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0 & -i\gamma k_x & -i\gamma k_y & a & 0 & 0\\
    -ik_x & 0&0&0& b & 0\\
    -ik_y & 0&0&0&0& b\\
    -\rho\omega^2 & 0&0&0& -ik_x & -ik_y\\
    0 & -\rho\omega^2 + \zeta k_x^2 + \mu k_y^2 & k_xk_y(\chi+\mu) & -ik_x\gamma & 0&0\\
    0 & k_xk_y(\chi+\mu) & -\rho\omega^2 + \zeta k_y^2 + \mu k_x^2 & -ik_y\gamma & 0&0
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
where, and this is a genuine trap in the thesis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shorthand}
  \gamma = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+2\mu},\quad
  a = \frac{1}{\lambda+2\mu},\quad
  b = \frac{1}{\mu},\quad
  \zeta = \frac{4\mu(\lambda+\mu)}{\lambda+2\mu},\quad
  \chi = \frac{2\mu\lambda}{\lambda+2\mu},
\end{equation}
with $\zeta-\chi = 2\mu$.  These are \emph{local} to~\eqref{eq:Asys}; the
thesis List of Symbols defines $\gamma,\zeta,\chi$ as Chapter~2 contrast
ratios, which do \emph{not} apply.  A footnote to this effect has been added to
the recompiled thesis.

\section{The wrapper under test}

\subsection{Its two operators}

\texttt{layered\_greens\_9x9} constructs a $9\times9$ from $\bG_6$ by
sandwiching it:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wrapper}
  \bM \;=\; \bA(\mathbf{k})\;\bG_6\;\bB .
\end{equation}

The \textbf{receiver-side operator} $\bA$ is
\texttt{strain\_from\_displacement\_traction}, a $9\times6$ mapping
$\mathbf{b}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\psi}$, i.e.\ $(u,t)\to(u,\varepsilon)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
\begin{aligned}
  \varepsilon_{zz} &= \frac{\sigma_{zz} - \lambda(ik_x u_x + ik_y u_y)}{\lambda+2\mu},
  &\quad \varepsilon_{xx} &= ik_x u_x, &\quad \varepsilon_{yy} &= ik_y u_y,\\
  2\varepsilon_{xy} &= ik_y u_x + ik_x u_y,
  &\quad 2\varepsilon_{zy} &= \sigma_{yz}/\mu, &\quad 2\varepsilon_{zx} &= \sigma_{xz}/\mu,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the displacements passed through unchanged.  Note $\bA$ depends on
$\mathbf{k}$ and never sees $k_z$: it recovers $\varepsilon_{zz}$ from
$\sigma_{zz}$ and the in-plane strains.

The \textbf{source-side operator} $\bB$ is, in the shipped code,
\texttt{traction\_from\_strain}: a $6\times9$, wavenumber-\emph{independent},
equal to $\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} \bI_3 & 0\\ 0 & PC\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$
with $C$ the isotropic Voigt stiffness and $P$ the traction-extraction.

\subsection{The corrections applied}

Two modifications were derived and applied, giving what is called
$\bM^{\text{corr}}$ below:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corr}
  \bM^{\text{corr}} = \bA(\mathbf{k})\ \bigl[\bG_6\,\bQ\bigr]\ \bB^{\dagger},
  \qquad
  \bQ = \operatorname{diag}\Bigl(1,-1,-1,\ \tfrac{i}{\omega},-\tfrac{i}{\omega},-\tfrac{i}{\omega}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  \bB^{\dagger} = -\bJ_6\,\bA_{\text{src}}(-\mathbf{k})^{T}\,\bW .
\end{equation}
$\bQ$ undoes the mixed basis on the source index; $\bB^{\dagger}$ was derived by
requiring the swap relation~\eqref{eq:gateE} below.  $\bA_{\text{src}}$ means
$\bA$ evaluated with the \emph{source} interface's elastic properties (using
the receiver's leaves an $83\%$ residual).

\section{The gates: exact statements and outcomes}

Each gate is a necessary condition.  Scripts named are in
\texttt{scripts/} of \texttt{MultipleScatteringCalculations}.

\subsection{GATE A --- the invariant of the closed form}\label{sec:gateA}

\textbf{Statement.}  $\bW\,\bP^{\text{cf}}(\mathbf{r})$ is symmetric.

\textbf{Origin.}  Measured, not assumed: the elementwise ratio
$P_{ab}/P_{ba}$ is an exact outer product $w_a/w_b$ with
$w = (1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2)$ --- the Voigt engineering factor
of~\eqref{eq:voigt}.

\textbf{Outcome.}  \textbf{PASS}, $4.2\times10^{-18}$ at four separations;
and $1.0\times10^{-16}$ over twelve further random separations, so it is an
identity rather than a coincidence.

\subsection{GATE F --- the same invariant, for the wrapper}

\textbf{Statement.}  In the homogeneous limit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gateF}
  \bW\,\bM(k_x,k_y) \ \text{is symmetric.}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Why this follows.}  $\bW\bP^{\text{cf}}(x,y,\Delta z)$ is symmetric at
every $(x,y)$; the $2$-D transform over $(x,y)$ is a linear combination with
\emph{scalar} coefficients; a linear combination of symmetric matrices is
symmetric.  Verified numerically as well: after transforming
$\bP^{\text{cf}}$ on a grid, $\bW\tilde{\bP}$ is symmetric to
$6.4\times10^{-16}$ across twenty wavenumbers.

\textbf{Restriction.}  Licensed only in the homogeneous limit, where swapping
source and receiver leaves the medium unchanged.  For a genuinely stratified
reference no within-matrix symmetry should be expected.

\textbf{Outcome.}  $\bM^{\text{corr}}$ \textbf{FAILS}, residual $0.40$--$0.81$
across two frequencies and two slownesses.

\subsection{GATE E --- the swap relation}\label{sec:gateE}

\textbf{Statement.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gateE}
  \bM(i\!\leftarrow\! j)(+\mathbf{k})
  \;=\; \bW^{-1}\,\bigl[\bM(j\!\leftarrow\! i)(-\mathbf{k})\bigr]^{T}\,\bW .
\end{equation}

\textbf{Outcome.}  $\bM^{\text{corr}}$ \textbf{PASSES} at
$9.7\times10^{-16}$.

\textbf{What it cannot see.}  \eqref{eq:gateE} relates two \emph{different}
matrices and is homogeneous of degree one in $\bM$.  It therefore constrains
neither the overall scale (any constant multiplies both sides equally) nor the
relation between the $G,C,H,S$ blocks \emph{within} one matrix.  Both blind
spots proved to be live.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Correction (13 September 2026): GATE~E is a \emph{false} gate.}  It is
not merely blind --- it is false for the correct propagator, and it was passing
\emph{because} of a defect.  GATE~F gives $\bM_1 = \bW^{-1}\bM_1^{T}\bW$ and
GATE~E gives $\bM_1 = \bW^{-1}\bM_2^{T}\bW$; together they force
$\bM_1 = \bM_2$, i.e.\ $\bP(\mathbf{r}) = \bP(-\mathbf{r})$.  But $C$ and $H$
are \emph{first} derivatives of the Green's tensor and are therefore \emph{odd}
in $\mathbf{r}$, so that can hold only if $C$ vanishes.  The two relations
differ by exactly a sign on the mixed blocks --- which is defect~D3.  Measured:
the \emph{exact} whole-space reference, with no implementation in it at all,
fails GATE~E by $1.4348\times10^{-1}$, identical to five figures to the
corrected $6\times6$, and passes at $7\times10^{-17}$ only when D3 is
reintroduced.  GATE~A is the arbiter, having been measured directly on the
validated closed form at twelve random separations.  Consequently the exit
criterion of \texttt{gate\_9x9\_source\_convention.py} (``non-zero unless A, E
and F all pass'') can never be met.
\end{quote}

\subsection{GATE D --- the reciprocity law of $\bG_6$ itself}

\textbf{Statement.}  With
$\bS\bD = \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-i\omega,+i\omega,+i\omega)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gateD}
  \bG_6(i\!\leftarrow\! j)(+\mathbf{k})
  = \frac{i}{\omega}\,\bS\bD\ \bJ_6\bigl[\bG_6(j\!\leftarrow\! i)(-\mathbf{k})\bigr]^{T}\bJ_6\ \bS\bD .
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-i\omega,-i\omega,-i\omega)$ is the
$\sigma/(-i\omega)$ mixed basis applied uniformly to P--SV and SH, and
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,1,-1,-1)$ is the parity of the $x,y$ components
under $\mathbf{k}\to-\mathbf{k}$.

\textbf{Outcome.}  \textbf{PASS}, $6.3\times10^{-16}$.  Obtained by solving for
the weight, not guessing: a joint rank-one fit over interface pairs,
slownesses and frequencies has residual $4.8\times10^{-15}$ with $\bJ_6$ and
$0.97$ with the identity, so the symplectic signature is required.

\paragraph{Trap.}  Recovering the SH entries requires $k_y\neq0$.  At $k_y=0$
the SH channel decouples, components $2$ and $5$ vanish identically, and a fit
returns an unconstrained value for them.

\section{The negative results}

These close off regions of the search space and are the substantive output.

\paragraph{N1. No $\bB$ in the adjoint family satisfies E and F together.}
For $\bB \in \{-\bJ_6\bA_{\text{src}}(-\mathbf{k})^{T}\bW,\
\bS\bJ_6\bA_{\text{src}}(+\mathbf{k})^{T}\bW,\ \bJ_6\bA^{T}\bW,\ \dots\}$ the
shipped choice gives E $=5.3\times10^{-16}$ with F $=0.81$, and every variant
improving F destroys E (best F $=0.26$ at E $=0.74$).
\emph{No $\bB$ ever could}: E and F are mutually exclusive (\S\ref{sec:gateE}).
``Every variant improving F destroys E'' was never a clue about $\bB$ --- it was
two incompatible gates pulling in opposite directions.  The family also
contained the right answer all along (\S\ref{sec:resolution}).

\paragraph{N2. GATE F reduces to a property of $\bG_6$ alone.}
If $\bB = \bA^{T}\bW$ then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduction}
  \bW\bM = (\bW\bA)\,\bG\,(\bW\bA)^{T},
\end{equation}
which is symmetric \emph{if and only if} $\bG$ is symmetric.  So under that
ansatz the question is not about $\bB$ at all.

\paragraph{N3. No diagonal source correction makes $\bG_6$ symmetric.}
Requiring $\bG_6\operatorname{diag}(q)$ symmetric gives $15$ homogeneous
equations in $6$ unknowns.  The null space does \emph{not} exist: the smallest
singular value ratio stalls at $4.9\times10^{-3}\to1.8\times10^{-3}$ over
$f=6\to48$~Hz, the achieved symmetry is only $3$--$7\times10^{-2}$, and the
fitted $q$ drifts with frequency ($0.48, 0.53, 0.56, 0.74$).  The system is
inconsistent.

\paragraph{N4. $\Asys$ is not the conversion needed here.}
Every grouping $\bG\bQ\Asys^{-1}$, $\bG\bQ\Asys$, $\Asys^{-1}\bG\bQ$ leaves the
symmetry residual at $1.4$, no better than raw $\bG$.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Correction (13 September 2026).}  The version of this paragraph dated
27~July justified the $\Asys$ lead by asserting that Box~5.3 of the thesis
gives the state-jump $\leftrightarrow$ body-force equivalence as
$F = \Asys S$.  \emph{It does not.}  Box~5.3 assumes a source carrying both a
$\delta$ and a $\delta'$ part,
$\partial_z \mathbf{b} = \Asys\mathbf{b} + F_1\delta(z-z_S) + F_2\delta'(z-z_S)$
(the form of \emph{Kennett}~1983, Eq.~2.78), and gives the \emph{effective}
$\delta$ coefficient as $F_1 + \Asys F_2$.  So $\Asys$ multiplies the
$\delta'$ coefficient, and the object is a source vector, not a conversion
applied to a finished Green's function.  Writing
$\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b}_{\text{reg}} + F_2\delta$ gives
$\partial_z\mathbf{b}_{\text{reg}} - \Asys\mathbf{b}_{\text{reg}}
= (F_1 + \Asys F_2)\delta$: the $\delta$ part passes straight through to the
jump, and the $\delta'$ part enters multiplied by $\Asys$.  The measurement
above is sound; the mechanism it refuted was never the thesis's.  The thesis is
correct and its Box~5.3 in fact \emph{validates} the operator $\bB$ derived in
\S\ref{sec:resolution} --- see~\eqref{eq:box53}.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{N5. The lateral and stratified propagators are not commensurable
as built.}  In the homogeneous limit the ratio of the stratified spectral
kernel to the verified lattice kernel varies by $10$--$14\times$ across
wavenumber and \emph{grows} under damping, so it is not surface leakage.
Discretising the stratified propagator onto the lattice improves this
$3.8\times$ (spread $9.8\to2.55$) but does not close it; the residual is a
roughly constant factor near $12$ with $\sim200\%$ scatter.

\section{The question, as it stood}

Precisely one question, and it was thought to be small:

\begin{quote}
Given a $6\times6$ Green's function whose source index is a \emph{state jump}
in the mixed basis of \S2.2, what is the correct $6\times9$ source-side
operator $\bB$ such that $\bA\bG_6\bB$ reproduces the whole-space
$\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}G&C\\H&S\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$
of~\eqref{eq:Pcf}?
\end{quote}

It was the wrong question.  $\bB$ was never the defect.  N2 was the true
signal and it was under-used: \emph{GATE~F is a question about $\bG_6$}.

\section{The resolution}\label{sec:resolution}

\subsection{$\bB$, from the governing equation}

Write the true traction as $T_{ij} = c_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{kl} - m_{ij}$ with
$m$ the stress glut and $f$ the body force.  Setting
$f_i = F_i\delta(z-z_S)$ and $m_{ij} = M_{ij}\delta(z-z_S)$ in the first-order
system and integrating across $z_S$ gives the classical source discontinuity
vector, $[\mathbf{b}] \equiv \mathbf{b}(z_S^+)-\mathbf{b}(z_S^-)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump}
\begin{aligned}
  {[u_z]} &= a M_{zz}, &\quad [T_{zz}] &= -F_z,\\
  {[u_x]} &= b M_{xz}, &\quad [T_{xz}] &= -F_x + ik_x(M_{xx}-\gamma M_{zz}) + ik_y M_{xy},\\
  {[u_y]} &= b M_{yz}, &\quad [T_{yz}] &= -F_y + ik_y(M_{yy}-\gamma M_{zz}) + ik_x M_{xy},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $a,b,\gamma$ as in~\eqref{eq:shorthand}.  Two features of this kill the
search that was actually run: it is \emph{wavenumber-dependent}, and it mixes
the glut into \emph{both} halves of the state, so it can never be a diagonal
rescaling of anything.  In the slot convention the code actually uses
(engineering doubling on the shear stress slots), \eqref{eq:jump} is, entry for
entry,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bclosed}
  \bB \;=\; \bJ_6\,\bA_{\text{src}}(-\mathbf{k})^{T}\,\bW,
\end{equation}
which is the shipped $\bB^{\dagger}$ of~\eqref{eq:corr} \emph{up to a global
sign} --- and both GATE~E and GATE~F are blind to a global sign.  Hence N1.

\paragraph{The thesis validates this exactly.}  Box~5.3 works a point explosion
through its own machinery and obtains the effective source coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:box53}
  F_1 + \mathbf{A}_{S} F_2 = M(\omega)e^{-ik_x x_S}\delta(z-z_S)
  \left[\tfrac{1}{\rho\alpha^{2}},\,0,\,0,\,0,\,
  \tfrac{2ik_x\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}},\,
  \tfrac{2ik_y\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right]^{T}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:jump} with $M_{ij}=M\delta_{ij}$ gives
$[u_z] = aM = M/(\rho\alpha^2)$ and
$[T_{xz}] = ik_xM(1-\gamma) = 2ik_xM\beta^2/\alpha^2$.  Relative difference:
$\mathbf{0.000\times10^{0}}$, on all six components.  Two derivations thirty
years apart agreeing exactly.

\subsection{Three defects}

Let $\hat{\mathbf{k}} = (k_x,k_y)/|\mathbf{k}_\parallel|$,
$P^{\parallel}_{ab} = \hat{k}_a\hat{k}_b$ and
$P^{\perp} = \bI_2 - P^{\parallel}$.  Define, in the $(z,x,y)$ frame of \S2.1,
acting on $(\sigma_{zz},\sigma_{xz},\sigma_{yz})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K}
  \bK \;=\; 1 \,\oplus\, \bigl(-P^{\parallel} + P^{\perp}\bigr)
      \;=\; 1 \,\oplus\, \bigl(\bI_2 - 2\hat{\mathbf{k}}\hat{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathsf T}\bigr),
\end{equation}
a \emph{Householder reflection} about the plane perpendicular to the horizontal
propagation direction: purely geometric, involutive, and independent of the
medium and of frequency.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Corrected 14 September 2026.}  Equation \eqref{eq:K} previously carried a
factor $\eta_S$, the vertical $S$ slowness, on the perpendicular component.  That
factor was not physics.  It compensated a missing $\eta$ in the layered $SH$
eigenvector of the stratified solver, whose traction/displacement ratio was $\mu$
where an $SH$ wave requires $\mu\eta$.  The two errors cancel \emph{exactly} in a
uniform medium --- there every eigenvector comes from one layer --- so every gate
in this document passed at $10^{-15}$ while the \emph{layered} $SH$ reflection was
angle-independent, $(\mu_1-\mu_2)/(\mu_1+\mu_2)$, instead of the Aki \& Richards
form $(\mu_1\eta_1-\mu_2\eta_2)/(\mu_1\eta_1+\mu_2\eta_2)$.  A reflection
coefficient that does not depend on the ray parameter is the tell.  The two
halves are lethal apart: correcting either alone takes the uniform-limit
reduction from $10^{-15}$ to $\sim\!3\times10^{-1}$.  $P$ and $SV$ were correct
throughout.  See \texttt{scripts/gate\_sh\_impedance.py}, which holds the uniform
limit and the interface reflection simultaneously.
\end{quote}

\item[D1.] \texttt{assemble\_greens\_6x6} rescales the stress source columns on
  the displacement rows only, so the stress-row $\times$ stress-column block
  carries \emph{one} factor of $(-i\omega)$ where a consistent change of basis
  $\bD_{\text{row}}\bG\bD_{\text{col}}$ requires two.  Fix:
  $\bG_6[3\!:\!6,3\!:\!6] \times (-i\omega)$.
\item[D2.] The traction half of \emph{both} indices carries $\bK$: as $\bK$ on

\paragraph{Why the search could not have succeeded.}  $\bK$ carries
$K_{xy} = K_{yx} = -2k_xk_y/|\mathbf{k}_\parallel|^2$, two thirds of its weight
off-diagonal whenever $k_xk_y\neq0$.  A diagonal ansatz in $(z,x,y)$ is
inconsistent by construction --- which is exactly N3's ``$15$ equations, $6$
unknowns, no null space''.  $\bK$ is diagonal only on the two axes $k_y=0$ and
$k_x=0$.  (In the superseded $\eta_S$ form the off-diagonal entry was
$-(k_xk_y/|\mathbf{k}_\parallel|^2)(1+\eta_S)$, and the additional
slowness dependence made a constant fit hopeless as well.)

\paragraph{The $1/\omega$ residual was a mirage.}  With $\bG_6$ exact, GATE~F
still failed at a clean $1/\omega$ ($0.148$, $0.0403$, $0.0101$ at $f=12,48,192$
Hz).  Decomposing the antisymmetric part by block gives $0$ on $G$, $10^{-16}$
on $S$, and \emph{exactly} $2.0000$ on $C$ and on $H$ --- meaning $C = -H^{T}$.
That is a sign, not a $1/\omega$ term.  It presents as $1/\omega$ only because
$\|C\|,\|H\|$ are $1/\omega$-sized against $\|S\|$, which dominates the
normalising denominator.  Searching for a missing $1/\omega$ term in $\bQ$ could
not have worked.

\paragraph{GATE D, and the thesis.}  The corrected $\bG_6$ obeys
$\bG(i\!\leftarrow\!j)(+\mathbf{k}) =
\bJ_6[\bG(j\!\leftarrow\!i)(-\mathbf{k})]^{T}\bJ_6$ with \emph{no weight
whatsoever} --- not the $(\mp i\omega)^{\pm1}$ factors of~\eqref{eq:gateD}, and
not even the parity.  This is the law implied by the thesis's own construction
$\bG = \bD_z(\mathbf{k})\bQ^{z}\bD_z^{T}(-\mathbf{k})\bJ_6$ together with
$\bD_z^{-1} = -i\bJ_6\bD_z^{T}(-\mathbf{k})\bJ_6$, and it is Wapenaar's
unified $\bG = \bN\bG^{T}\bN$ verbatim.  The thesis had the clean law all
along; the implementation departed from it.

\section{What remains open}

\paragraph{The stratified extension --- CLOSED.}  $\bK$ needs no stratified
generalisation; it is purely geometric.  The apparent stratified failure
($0.009$--$0.398$ on the marine model) was a \emph{placement} artefact: every
interface of that model separates different materials and the test pairs sat
\emph{on} them, where the local medium is two-valued and the construction reads
a single one per plane.  (At the time $\bK$ itself was built on the local
$\eta_S$, which sharpened the same objection; the restriction survives the
removal of $\eta_S$ because the rest of the construction still reads one medium
per plane.)
With the planes in a layer interior and a fast slab crossed twice between them
at $Q=1000$ --- transmission, reflection, conversion and interbed multiples all
present --- the clean law holds to $1.7\times10^{-15}$ over nine geometries.
The control is included: on a discontinuity, no choice of side does better than
$3.9\times10^{-3}$.  The restriction costs the application nothing, since
scattering voxels live in layer interiors; it is enforced with a diagnostic in
\texttt{cubic\_scattering.layered\_correction}.

\paragraph{Upstream or here? --- SETTLED by experiment.}  Applying D1 in-process
and running the sibling repository's own suite gives $119$ passed at baseline
and $118$ passed / $1$ failed with the fix.  The single failure is not a
physical check: it asserts that the $9\times9$ solver agrees with the $4\times4$
solver, where the two sides are $(\bA\bG_6\bB)$ driven by $T_{9}$ and
$\bG_{4}$ driven by $\bB T_{9}\bA$.  Both wrap the \emph{same} raw Green's
function with the \emph{same} $\bA$ and $\bB$, so it is an algebraic identity
that holds for \emph{any} $\bG$ and cannot see a common-mode error --- it would
fail just as readily for a correct change.  Verdict: carry the correction here,
for a sharper reason than the original one.  Not ``the error cancels in $R$ and
$T$'', but \emph{no upstream test compares the $9\times9$ path to anything
outside itself}.  Raise a note, not a patch.

\paragraph{N5 --- improved, not closed.}  Re-taken through the corrected
wrapper, the kernel-ratio spread roughly halves (pair $2\!\to\!0$ at $Q=3$:
$34.9 \to 5.9$) and still does not fall with $Q$.  So the wrapper defects were a
real part of N5 but not the whole of it.  The remainder is the discrete-versus-
continuum aliasing already diagnosed: \texttt{\_build\_slab\_kernels} sums the
continuum propagator over discrete voxel positions and transforms, so by Poisson
summation its spectral kernel is the continuum kernel \emph{aliased} over
reciprocal lattice vectors, while \texttt{layered\_greens} returns the un-aliased
kernel at a single $\mathbf{k}$.  The consequence is unchanged and is the next
piece of real work: \textbf{lattice-sum $\bP^{z}$ into the discrete
representation before adding it to $\bP^{x}$}.  That is a construction, not a
rescaling.

\paragraph{A withdrawn result, recorded.}  An attempt to confirm the aliasing
account by comparing the corrected kernel against a \emph{freshly written}
continuum $9\times9$ showed a uniform $20\%$ offset at every propagating
wavenumber, flat in frequency --- while the same construction agrees with the
\emph{validated} whole-space reference to $5\times10^{-15}$.  The fresh
reference was therefore the suspect, and the comparison was withdrawn rather
than reported.  This is the second time in this work that an unvalidated
reference nearly produced a false result; the first was a wavenumber grid that
did not contain the $S$ pole ($k_S = \omega/\beta$), which made the absolute
reference of \S\ref{sec:resolution} look broken at $O(1)$ until the grid was
widened.  Validate the reference before believing the comparison.

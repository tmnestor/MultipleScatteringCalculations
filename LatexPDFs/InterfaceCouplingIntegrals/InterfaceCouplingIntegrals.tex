\large The Static Image in Closed Form, and the Plane Waves for the Rest}
\author{T. M. Nestor\\[2pt]\small Independent researcher, Australia}
\date{Draft, 11 October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In a stratified elastic reference medium the Green's tensor is that of a homogeneous medium plus the waves
reflected and transmitted at the interfaces, and it is most naturally available not as a tabulated function
but through its plane-wave, up- and down-going, factorisation. A volume-integral method that divides the
heterogeneity into cubic cells carrying polynomial fields needs the Galerkin coupling of every pair of
cells through this reference. Between cells in planes that do not touch, the plane-wave representation
with the cells' moments in closed form is exact, and its integral over the lateral wavenumber converges
exponentially. Next to a welded interface it does not: a cell is coupled to itself and to its neighbours
through its own mirror image, which touches them, and the reflected coupling converges only algebraically.
We show that the singular part is the static field of a point force at the interface, to which the
reflected and transmitted kernels tend as $(\omega/\beta q)^2$ at large lateral wavenumber $q$, and obtain it and
its coupling blocks in closed form. Solved in the lateral-wavenumber domain, Rongved's problem gives every
entry as $\ee^{-q\zeta}/q$ times a polynomial; in space the reflected and transmitted static fields are $356$
and $275$ terms built on Kelvin's potential and two logarithmic potentials, Mindlin's terms, which at a
welded interface are present for every pair of distinct media and which we write as a line of images. The
moments of these kernels over a box with the singular point at a vertex reduce, by Euler's identity for
homogeneous functions, to faces, edges and edges to infinity, with Hadamard finite parts where the degree is
negative and two base integrals where it is zero,
$B_1=\tfrac54\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)-G$ and $B_2=\tfrac13\log[(1+\sqrt2)^2/(2(1+\sqrt3))]$, so that every moment
is elementary apart from Catalan's constant and one value of Clausen's function. The corner moments and
the blocks agree with independent routes to $10^{-15}$ and $10^{-13}$ or better, coincide with the blocks of the homogeneous medium
to $2$ to $8\times10^{-15}$ when the two media are the same, and with the static part subtracted the
plane-wave remainder converges as $Q^{-4}$ in the cutoff $Q$ instead of $Q^{-2}$. The next term of the
kernels, of order $\omega^2$ with a coefficient that does not depend on the frequency, is derived in the
wavenumber domain; subtracted as well, it leaves a remainder that converges as $Q^{-6}$, so that a
double-precision plane-wave integral truncated at $Qh=10$ gives the block of a touching cell to
$4\times10^{-10}$ at $\omega h/\beta=1/6$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A volume-integral method for elastic waves represents a heterogeneous body as a contrast with a reference
medium, divides the contrast into cells, and couples every pair of cells through the Green's tensor of the
reference. When the reference is homogeneous the coupling of cubic cells carrying polynomial fields can be
obtained in closed form and evaluated to round-off \citep{NestorCoupling2026}. In seismology the natural
reference is stratified, and its Green's tensor is that of a homogeneous medium plus waves reflected and
transmitted at the interfaces. The stratified Green's tensor is rarely tabulated: it is computed through its
plane-wave representation, in which, at each lateral wavenumber, waves travel up and down through the layers
and are reflected and transmitted at each interface \citep{FuchsMuller1971,Kennett1983}. A volume method built
on a stratified reference then needs the coupling blocks in a form that uses only this up- and down-going
factorisation \citep{Nestor1996}; tabulating the Green's tensor between all pairs of depths would cost
storage quadratic in the number of depth planes, where the plane waves need only linear storage.

For cells in different planes that do not touch, the plane-wave representation with the cells' moments in
closed form is exact, and its integral over the lateral wavenumber converges exponentially because the
evanescent waves decay between the planes. For cells in touching planes it diverges, and those blocks
are the closed forms of the homogeneous medium \citep{NestorCoupling2026}. At an interface the same failure
reaches further. The reflection of a cell's field is the field of its mirror image, and the image of a cell
that touches the interface touches the cell itself and its neighbours along the interface. The reflected
coupling of every such cell is therefore singular, and its plane-wave integral converges only
algebraically.

This paper removes that obstacle. Its contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The plane-wave representation of the coupling blocks, stated for the direct, reflected and
  transmitted couplings with the cells' moments in closed form (Section~\ref{sec:planewaves}).
  \item The identification of the singular part of the reflected and transmitted couplings as the static
  field of a point force at a welded interface (Rongved's problem), with the dynamic kernels tending to it
  as $(\omega/\beta q)^2$; subtracting it changes the convergence of the plane-wave remainder from $Q^{-2}$ to
  $Q^{-4}$ (Sections~\ref{sec:planewaves} and~\ref{sec:static}).
  \item The static image and the transmitted static field in space, as $356$ and $275$ canonical terms on
  three potentials, with Mindlin's logarithmic terms written as a line of images (Section~\ref{sec:static}).
  \item The coupling blocks of these fields in closed form: the moments over a box with the singular point at a
  vertex reduce to elementary functions and two constants, $B_1$ and $B_2$, which we evaluate in closed
  form (Sections~\ref{sec:blocks} and~\ref{sec:transmission}).
  \item The term of order $\omega^2$ of the reflected and transmitted kernels, by perturbation of the static
  problem, and its subtraction in the wavenumber domain with a frequency-independent coefficient; the
  remainder then converges as $Q^{-6}$, and a cutoff low enough for double-precision mode sums suffices
  (Section~\ref{sec:omega2}).
  \item Verification of each step against routes that share no step with it (Section~\ref{sec:verification}).
\end{enumerate}
The dynamic remainder, smooth by construction, is left to the plane waves; Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
describes how they carry it through a stack of layers.

\section{Cells, blocks and the plane-wave representation}\label{sec:planewaves}

\paragraph{Cells and blocks.} A cell is a cube of half-width $h$ centred at $\bx_c$, with local coordinates
$\bxi=(\bx-\bx_c)/h\in[-1,1]^3$. It carries a nine-component state, the displacement and an independent
engineering strain, as a polynomial in $\bxi$ \citep{NestorLegendre2026}; the field is tested against products
of Legendre polynomials $L_a(\bxi)=\prod_iP_{l_{a,i}}(\xi_i)$, and the source density, the contrast times the
state, is written in the monomials $m_c(\bxi')=\prod_i\xi_i'^{\,e_{c,i}}$. With $\bP(\bx,\bx')$ the
$9\times9$ point propagator of the reference medium, which maps a point source of force and engineering
stress at $\bx'$ to the displacement and engineering strain at $\bx$, a field cell $V_m$ and a source cell
$V_n$ couple through the blocks
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
  \bK_{ac}=\int_{V_m}\!\int_{V_n}L_a(\bxi)\,\bP(\bx,\bx')\,m_c(\bxi')\,\dd^3x\,\dd^3x'.
\end{equation}
In a homogeneous medium $\bP$ depends on $\bx-\bx'$ only, and with $\bx=\bx_m+h\bxi$, $\bx'=\bx_n+h\bxi'$ the
block becomes an integral over the separation $\bs=h(\bxi-\bxi')\in[-2h,2h]^3$ against the weight
$W_{ac}(\bs)=\prod_iw_{l_{a,i}e_{c,i}}(s_i)$, with $w_{\alpha\beta}(s)=h\int v^\alpha(v-s/h)^\beta\,\dd v$ the
cross-correlation of the two cells' one-dimensional factors, a piecewise polynomial on eight boxes
\citep{NestorCoupling2026}. The kernel is singular at $\bs=-(\bx_m-\bx_n)$, which for touching cells is a
vertex of some of the boxes.

\paragraph{The plane-wave representation.} Take coordinates in the order $(z,x,y)$, $z$ the depth, and a
lateral wavenumber $\bk=(k_x,k_y)\in\mathbb R^2$, $q=|\bk|$. For each wave $\nu\in\{\mathrm P,\mathrm{SV},\mathrm{SH}\}$
of speed $c_\nu$ ($c_{\mathrm P}=\alpha$, $c_{\mathrm{SV}}=c_{\mathrm{SH}}=\beta$) let
\[
  k_{z,\nu}=\bigl(\omega^2/c_\nu^2-q^2\bigr)^{1/2},\quad\operatorname{Im}k_{z,\nu}\ge0,\qquad
  \bk_\nu^\sigma=(\sigma k_{z,\nu},k_x,k_y),\quad\sigma=\pm1,
\]
the wave vector travelling down ($\sigma=+1$) or up ($\sigma=-1$). Its polarisations are
\[
  \be_{\mathrm P}^\sigma=\frac{\alpha}{\omega}\,\bk_{\mathrm P}^\sigma,\qquad
  \be_{\mathrm{SH}}=\frac{(0,-k_y,k_x)}{q},\qquad
  \be_{\mathrm{SV}}^\sigma=\frac{\beta}{\omega}\,\bk_{\mathrm{SV}}^\sigma\times\be_{\mathrm{SH}},
\]
with $\be_{\mathrm{SH}}=(0,1,0)$ at $\bk=\mathbf 0$. Each is a unit vector in the bilinear sense, $\be\cdot\be=1$,
which, unlike $\be\cdot\bar\be=1$, continues analytically into the evanescent range $q>\omega/c_\nu$. The
state of the wave is the nine-vector $\bd_\nu^\sigma=(\be,\boldsymbol\varepsilon)$ of its displacement and
engineering strain, $\varepsilon_{ij}=\tfrac{\ii}{2}(k_ie_j+k_je_i)$ with $\bk=\bk_\nu^\sigma$ and the three
shears doubled, and $\bM=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,1,1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ pairs the
engineering-stress entries of a source with the engineering-strain entries of a state. In a homogeneous
medium of density $\rho$, for $\sigma(z-z')>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modeprop}
  \bP(\bx,\bx')=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\sum_\nu w_\nu\,
  \bd_\nu^\sigma\,\bd_\nu^{\sigma\mathsf T}\bM\,
  \ee^{\ii\bk_\nu^\sigma\cdot(\bx-\bx')}\,\dd^2k,\qquad
  w_\nu=\frac{\ii}{2\rho c_\nu^2k_{z,\nu}},
\end{equation}
the source entering through $\bd_\nu^{\sigma\mathsf T}\bM$ and the field through $\bd_\nu^\sigma$, as reciprocity
requires. For cells in planes that do not touch, $|z_m-z_n|\ge4h$, set $\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(z_m-z_n)$;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:block} then becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modeblock}
  \bK_{ac}=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\sum_\nu w_\nu\,
  \Lambda_a\bigl(\bk_\nu^\sigma\bigr)\,\mu_c\bigl(-\bk_\nu^\sigma\bigr)\,
  \bd_\nu^\sigma\,\bd_\nu^{\sigma\mathsf T}\bM\,
  \ee^{\ii\bk_\nu^\sigma\cdot(\bx_m-\bx_n)}\,\dd^2k,
\end{equation}
with the scalar moments of the two cells, both in closed form for complex arguments \citep{NestorCoupling2026},
\begin{align}
  \Lambda_a(\bkappa)&=h^3\!\int_{[-1,1]^3}\!L_a(\bxi)\,\ee^{\ii h\bkappa\cdot\bxi}\,\dd^3\xi
  =h^3\prod_{i}2\,\ii^{\,l_{a,i}}j_{l_{a,i}}(h\kappa_i),\label{eq:Lambda}\\
  \mu_c(\bkappa)&=h^3\!\int_{[-1,1]^3}\!m_c(\bxi')\,\ee^{\ii h\bkappa\cdot\bxi'}\,\dd^3\xi'
  =h^3\prod_iF_{e_{c,i}}(h\kappa_i),\qquad F_n(t)=\int_{-1}^1\xi^n\ee^{\ii t\xi}\,\dd\xi,\label{eq:mu}
\end{align}
$j_l$ the spherical Bessel functions and $F_n$ a finite combination of them.

\paragraph{Reflection and transmission.} Let medium A, with Lam\'e constants $\lambda_A$, $\mu_A$ and density
$\rho_A$, fill $z<0$ and medium B fill $z>0$, welded at $z=0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:interface}). With the
amplitudes referred to the interface and the polarisations of each medium, a down-going wave $\nu$ in A
gives the up-going wave $\mu$ in A with amplitude $R_{\mu\nu}(\bk)$ and the down-going wave $\mu$ in B with
amplitude $T_{\mu\nu}(\bk)$, obtained at each $\bk$ from the continuity of displacement and traction, a
$6\times6$ linear system \citep{Kennett1983}. For $\bx$ and $\bx'$ both in A the reflected propagator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflected}
  \bP^{R}(\bx,\bx')=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\sum_{\mu,\nu}
  \bd_\mu^{A-}\,R_{\mu\nu}\,w_\nu^A\,\bd_\nu^{A+\mathsf T}\bM\,
  \ee^{\ii(\bk_\mu^{A-}\cdot\bx-\bk_\nu^{A+}\cdot\bx')}\,\dd^2k,
\end{equation}
and for $\bx'$ in A and $\bx$ in B the whole coupling is the transmitted one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transmitted}
  \bP^{T}(\bx,\bx')=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\sum_{\mu,\nu}
  \bd_\mu^{B+}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,w_\nu^A\,\bd_\nu^{A+\mathsf T}\bM\,
  \ee^{\ii(\bk_\mu^{B+}\cdot\bx-\bk_\nu^{A+}\cdot\bx')}\,\dd^2k,
\end{equation}
the superscripts naming the medium whose wave numbers and polarisations are used. The blocks follow as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:modeblock}, with $\Lambda_a(\bk_\mu^{\pm})\,\mu_c(-\bk_\nu^{+})$ in place of
$\Lambda_a\mu_c$ and the phase $\ee^{\ii(\bk_\mu\cdot\bx_m-\bk_\nu\cdot\bx_n)}$. The vertical phase of the
reflected term is $\ee^{\ii(k_{z,\mu}|z|+k_{z,\nu}|z'|)}$: its decay on the evanescent branch is set by the
distance of the receiver from the source's mirror image $\bx'^*=(-z',x',y')$.

\paragraph{Where the plane waves fail.} For cells in touching planes, $|z_m-z_n|=2h$, the integral
\eqref{eq:modeblock} does not converge: on the evanescent branch $k_{z,\nu}=\ii\kappa$, $\kappa\to\infty$, the
moments $\Lambda_a$ and $\mu_c$ grow as $\ee^{\kappa h}$ each and cancel the decay $\ee^{-2\kappa h}$ of the
phase. In the reflected coupling the relevant distance is that from the image, $|z_m+z_n|$, which is $2h$
for any two cells that touch the interface, and the same cancellation occurs (Figure~\ref{fig:interface}a).
The integrand then decays only algebraically in $q$, and so does the error of a truncated integral.
Table~\ref{tab:subtraction} shows it for a cell that touches the interface, coupled to its own image: the
contribution of each shell $Q<qh<2Q$ falls by a factor of four from one shell to the next, so the error of an
integral truncated at $Q$ falls as $Q^{-2}$.

The singular part of the integrand is its behaviour at large $q$. There $k_{z,\nu}=\ii q\,[1-\omega^2/(2c_\nu^2q^2)
+O(q^{-4})]$, and the reflected and transmitted kernels tend to the static kernel of Section~\ref{sec:static}.
At fixed depths the difference is of relative order $\omega^2\zeta/(c^2q)$, since the correction to $k_{z,\nu}$
multiplies the depth $\zeta$ in the phase; in the block of cells that touch the interface the depths that
matter are of order $1/q$, and the difference of the integrands is of relative order $(\omega/\beta q)^2$
(about $2(\omega/\beta q)^2$ for the cell of Table~\ref{tab:subtraction}, from $qh=20$ to $1280$). Writing
$\bK^{R}=\bK^{R}_0+(\bK^{R}-\bK^{R}_0)$, with $\bK^{R}_0$ the block of the static image, the first term is obtained
in closed form (Section~\ref{sec:blocks}) and the second by the plane-wave integral with the static spectrum
subtracted under the integral sign. The remainder converges as $Q^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:subtraction}): the
subtraction gains exactly the two orders of $(\omega/\beta q)^2$, and at $Qh=10$ it leaves $1.3\times10^{-6}$ of the
block where the truncated integral leaves $8\times10^{-3}$.

The kernels must be evaluated with care at large $q$. Written as a sum over the modes $\nu$, as in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:reflected} and \eqref{eq:transmitted}, the P and SV terms cancel as $(cq/\omega)^4$, because their
bilinear-unit polarisations grow as $cq/\omega$ while their sum tends to the finite static kernel. In double
precision the reflected kernel is then in error by $6\times10^{-7}$ at $qh=20$ for the cell of
Table~\ref{tab:subtraction}, and above $qh\approx100$ the remainder after the subtraction is mostly rounding
error. Table~\ref{tab:subtraction} was therefore computed with the kernels in $40$-digit arithmetic, which agree
with $60$ digits to $3\times10^{-16}$ at $qh=640$. A production evaluation in double precision therefore needs a cutoff below
$qh\approx20$; Section~\ref{sec:omega2} removes the next term of the expansion so that such a cutoff is
accurate enough.

\section{The static field of a point force at a welded interface}\label{sec:static}

\paragraph{In the lateral-wavenumber domain.} Let a unit point force act in A at depth $z'<0$. In the frame
whose $x$ axis lies along $\bk$, the static Navier equations of an isotropic medium have the solutions
$(a+bz)\,\ee^{\pm qz}$, the double root replacing the separate P and S waves of the dynamic problem. The
field in A is Kelvin's field plus a reflected field regular as $z\to-\infty$; the field in B is transmitted
and regular as $z\to\infty$; displacement and traction on the planes $z=\mathrm{const}$ are continuous at
$z=0$. This is the problem of \citet{Rongved1955}, solved here in the lateral-wavenumber domain. Its
solution has a simple structure: every entry of the reflected tensor is $\ee^{q(z+z')}/q$ times a
polynomial of degree at most one in each of $qz$ and $qz'$, and every entry of the transmitted tensor is
$\ee^{-q(z-z')}/q$ times a polynomial in $qz$ and $qz'$, the coefficients rational in the Lam\'e
constants. When the two media coincide the reflected tensor vanishes and the transmitted one is Kelvin's.

\paragraph{In space.} With $\zeta=-(z+z')>0$ for the reflected field and $\zeta=z-z'>0$ for the transmitted
one, $\brho$ the lateral separation and $R=(|\brho|^2+\zeta^2)^{1/2}$, the transform to space follows from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:imagetransform}
  \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\ee^{\ii\bk\cdot\brho}\,q^{m}\ee^{-q\zeta}\,\dd^2k
  =\begin{cases}(-\partial_\zeta)^{m+1}\Phi_0,&m\ge-1,\\ \Phi_1,&m=-2,\\ \Phi_2,&m=-3,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with the three potentials
\begin{equation}\label{eq:potentials}
  \Phi_0=\frac{1}{2\pi R},\qquad\Phi_1=-\frac{\log(R+\zeta)}{2\pi},\qquad\Phi_2=\frac{\zeta\log(R+\zeta)-R}{2\pi},
\end{equation}
each the $\zeta$-antiderivative of the one before, $\partial_\zeta\Phi_1=-\Phi_0$ and
$\partial_\zeta\Phi_2=-\Phi_1$. Rotating from the frame of $\bk$ to fixed axes brings factors $k_h/q$; since
$\ii k_h$ becomes $\partial_{\rho_h}$, an entry $(k_h/q)f(q)$ is $\partial_{\rho_h}$ of the transform of
$f/(\ii q)$, and an entry $(k_hk_l/q^2)f(q)$ is $-\partial_{\rho_h}\partial_{\rho_l}$ of the transform of
$f/q^2$. These divisions by $q$ are what produce the powers $m=-2$ and $-3$, and so the logarithmic
potentials. They are the terms that \citet{Mindlin1936} found for the half-space with a free surface; at a
welded interface they are present for every pair of media, with coefficients that vanish only when the
media coincide.

\paragraph{The potentials as finite parts.} The ladder continues below $m=-3$, and the next order in the
frequency (Section~\ref{sec:omega2}) needs it. For $m>-2$ the transform is the Laplace transform of a Bessel
function \citep{GradshteynRyzhik2007},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplacehankel}
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty q^{m+1}\ee^{-q\zeta}J_0(q\rho)\,\dd q
  =\frac{\Gamma(m+2)}{2\pi}\,R^{-m-2}\,P_{m+1}\!\Bigl(\frac{\zeta}{R}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
$P_\nu$ the Legendre function of the first kind, and at $m=-1$ it is $\Phi_0$. At $m=-1-j$, $j\ge1$, the
integral diverges at $q=0$ and $\Gamma$ has a pole. We define $\Phi_j$ as Hadamard's finite part, the
coefficient of $\varepsilon^0$ in the Laurent series of Eq.~\eqref{eq:laplacehankel} at $m=-1-j+\varepsilon$:
the analytic continuation in the power of $q$, the same convention by which Euler's identity continues the
corner moments in the degree (Section~\ref{sec:blocks}). With $n=j-1$, $P_{-j+\varepsilon}=P_{n-\varepsilon}$
and the derivative of $P_\nu$ in its degree at an integer,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:legendredegree}
  \frac{\partial P_\nu(x)}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{\nu=n}
  =P_n(x)\Bigl[\log\frac{1+x}{2}+2\psi(2n+1)-2\psi(n+1)\Bigr]
  +2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+k}\frac{2k+1}{(n-k)(n+k+1)}\,P_k(x),
\end{equation}
the finite part is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fppotentials}
  \Phi_j=\frac{(-1)^n}{2\pi\,n!}\Bigl\{\Bigl[c_j-\log\frac{R+\zeta}{2}\Bigr]R^nP_n\Bigl(\frac{\zeta}{R}\Bigr)
  -2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+k}\frac{2k+1}{(n-k)(n+k+1)}\,R^nP_k\Bigl(\frac{\zeta}{R}\Bigr)\Bigr\},
  \qquad c_j=3\psi(j)-2\psi(2j-1),
\end{equation}
$\psi$ the digamma function. Each $R^nP_k(\zeta/R)$ is a polynomial in $R$ and $\zeta$, so every potential is
a polynomial times $\log(R+\zeta)$ plus a polynomial; for instance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi34}
\begin{aligned}
  2\pi\,\Phi_3&=\Bigl(\tfrac14R^2-\tfrac34\zeta^2\Bigr)\Bigl(\log\tfrac{R+\zeta}{2}+\gamma\Bigr)
  -\tfrac14R^2+\tfrac34R\zeta+\tfrac14\zeta^2,\\
  2\pi\,\Phi_4&=\Bigl(\tfrac{5}{12}\zeta^3-\tfrac14R^2\zeta\Bigr)\Bigl(\log\tfrac{R+\zeta}{2}+\gamma\Bigr)
  +\tfrac19R^3+\tfrac14R^2\zeta-\tfrac{5}{12}R\zeta^2-\tfrac14\zeta^3,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$\gamma$ Euler's constant. The ladder $\partial_\zeta\Phi_{j+1}=-\Phi_j$ holds for the finite parts, since
differentiation under the integral commutes with the continuation, and every $\Phi_j$ is harmonic. The residue
at the pole is $(-1)^nR^nP_n(\zeta/R)/(2\pi\,n!)$, a solid harmonic: it is exactly the freedom that a cutoff
at small $q$ would leave in the transform, and the finite part fixes its coefficient. The potentials of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:potentials} differ from these only by such harmonics: the finite parts are
$\Phi_1+(\log2-\gamma)/2\pi$ and $\Phi_2-\zeta(\log2-\gamma)/2\pi$. Since $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ occur in the
static field only under at least one and two lateral derivatives respectively, which annihilate the
difference, the static blocks are the same with either. A term $\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_j$ needs the finite part at all only
when its lateral derivatives are fewer than $j$: its integrand then behaves as $q^{\alpha_x+\alpha_y-1-j}$ at
small $q$ and is not integrable in the plane.

\paragraph{Canonical terms.} The point propagator differentiates the Green's tensor: a receiver derivative
$\partial_z$ acts on the explicit $z$ and on $\zeta$, as $\partial/\partial z-\partial_\zeta$ for the image and
$\partial/\partial z+\partial_\zeta$ for the transmitted field, and similarly for source derivatives and the
lateral ones. Collected, the $9\times9$ static image is a sum of $356$ terms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:imageterms}
  c(\lambda_A,\mu_A,\lambda_B,\mu_B)\;z^{A}z'^{B}\;\partial_{\rho_x}^{\alpha_x}\partial_{\rho_y}^{\alpha_y}
  \partial_\zeta^{\alpha_z}\Phi_j(\brho,\zeta),
\end{equation}
in $61$ families $(j,\balpha)$: $\Phi_0$ with up to four derivatives, and $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ under lateral
derivatives only (one to four, and two to four), since a $\zeta$-derivative of a logarithmic potential is
the potential below it. The transmitted field has $275$ terms in $46$ families of the same kind. Every kernel
$\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_j$ is homogeneous in $(\brho,\zeta)$, of degree $-1-|\balpha|$, $-|\balpha|$ and
$1-|\balpha|$ for $j=0$, $1$, $2$.

\paragraph{A line of images.} Every logarithmic term carries a lateral derivative and therefore vanishes as
$\zeta\to\infty$. Integrating the relations between the potentials from $\zeta$ to infinity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lineofimages}
  \partial^{\balpha}\Phi_1(\brho,\zeta)=\int_\zeta^\infty\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_0(\brho,t)\,\dd t,\qquad
  \partial^{\balpha}\Phi_2(\brho,\zeta)=\int_\zeta^\infty(t-\zeta)\,\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_0(\brho,t)\,\dd t.
\end{equation}
Mindlin's terms are thus Kelvin's image spread along the vertical half-line beyond the image point, with
uniform and with linearly growing density (Figure~\ref{fig:images}). This is the form in which their moments
are computed.

\section{Coupling blocks with the static image}\label{sec:blocks}

\paragraph{The separation form.} For two cells in A the image kernel depends on the lateral differences
$\rho_h=x_h-x'_h$ but on the vertical sum $\zeta=-(z+z')$. The block is therefore an integral over
$(\rho_x,\rho_y,\zeta)$ against a weight that is a cross-correlation of the cells' factors in the two
lateral coordinates, as in the homogeneous medium, and a convolution in the vertical,
$u_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=\int v^\alpha(\tau-v)^\beta\,\dd v$ in $\tau=v+v'$, into which the factors $z^A$ and
$z'^B$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:imageterms} are expanded. The weights are piecewise polynomials with rational
coefficients, computed exactly. The domain divides into boxes on which the weight is a polynomial.

\paragraph{The singular point.} The image kernel is singular only at $\brho=0$, $\zeta=0$, which is reached
when both cells touch the interface and touch each other laterally (Figure~\ref{fig:interface}a), and then at
a vertex of the boxes that hold it. There the vertical weight vanishes as $\zeta^{1+A+B}$, and for every one
of the $356$ terms the integrand is at worst $O(R^{-2})$: it is absolutely integrable, with no distribution
and no derivative to be moved onto the weight. Boxes away from the singular point have smooth integrands
and are integrated by Gauss rules in coordinates centred on each box. On a box with the singular point at
a vertex, reflected onto the positive octant, every term requires the corner moments
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cornermoment}
  M_{j,\balpha}[p,q,r]=\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^b\!\!\int_0^c\rho_x^p\rho_y^q\zeta^r\,
  \partial^{\balpha}\Phi_j(\brho,\zeta)\,\dd\zeta\,\dd\rho_y\,\dd\rho_x,
\end{equation}
with $a=b=c=2h$. They are universal: by homogeneity they scale as a power of $h$ times a number independent
of $h$, the frequency and the media.

\paragraph{Kelvin's potential.} For $j=0$, $\partial^{\balpha}R^{-1}=P_{\balpha}(\brho,\zeta)\,R^{-2|\balpha|-1}$
with $P_{\balpha}$ a homogeneous polynomial of degree $|\balpha|$, so the moments are combinations of the master
integrals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boxI}
  I[p,q,r;n]=\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^b\!\!\int_0^c x^py^qz^r\,(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{n/2}\,\dd z\,\dd y\,\dd x,
\end{equation}
here for odd $n$ down to $-9$. The integrand is homogeneous of degree $p+q+r+n$ about the singular point,
and Euler's identity with the divergence theorem reduces the box to its three far faces, the faces through
the singular point carrying no flux; on a face the same step in two dimensions reduces to edges, and on an
edge the integral is elementary \citep{NestorCoupling2026}. One transcendental function enters, through the
face integral of $R^{-3}$: $\int_0^b\!\int_0^cR^{-3}\,\dd z\,\dd y=a^{-1}\arctan\bigl(bc/(a\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2})\bigr)$
on the face $x=a$, which is continued to lower $n$ by differentiation with respect to $a$.

\paragraph{The logarithmic potentials.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:lineofimages} the $\zeta$-integral of the box can be
done first, which leaves Kelvin's potential against a polynomial weight in $t$ over the box and over the
semi-infinite column $[0,a]\times[0,b]\times[c,\infty)$ above it. On $[0,c]$ the weight is $t^{r+1}/(r+1)$
($j=1$) or $t^{r+2}/((r+1)(r+2))$ ($j=2$); on the column it is $c^{r+1}/(r+1)$, or
$t\,c^{r+1}/(r+1)-c^{r+2}/(r+2)$. The column is the semi-infinite box less the finite one. Euler's identity
holds on the semi-infinite box, the face at infinity carrying no flux, and its faces reduce to edges that
run to infinity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lineinf}
  \int_0^\infty z^r(A^2+z^2)^{n/2}\,\dd z=\tfrac12\,A^{\,r+n+1}\,\mathrm B\Bigl(\tfrac{r+1}{2},-\tfrac{r+n+1}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
Beta functions at half-integers, elementary. Two cases need care. Where the degree $p+q+r+n+3$ of a column
term is negative, each of the two boxes holds an integral that diverges at the singular corner; Euler's
identity then gives Hadamard's finite part of each, and the two finite parts differ by the exact integral
over the column, because their singular contributions at the shared corner are equal. Where the degree is
zero the identity is degenerate, and the column is reduced instead by integration by parts in $t$, then in
$\rho_x$ and $\rho_y$, whose boundary terms are faces at height $c$ and strips at $\rho_x=a$ or $\rho_y=b$,
away from the singular point. Two base integrals remain:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B12}
  B_1=\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^b\!\!\int_c^\infty R^{-3}\,\dd t\,\dd\rho_y\,\dd\rho_x,\qquad
  B_2=\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^b\!\!\int_c^\infty\rho_x\rho_y\,R^{-5}\,\dd t\,\dd\rho_y\,\dd\rho_x .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The base integrals.} Both are of degree zero, so they depend only on the ratios of the sides,
which here are equal. The $t$-integrals are elementary, and in polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ in the
$\brho$-plane so are the radial ones: with $c=1$ and $s=\sqrt{1+r^2}$,
\[
  B_1=\int\!\!\log\frac{1+s}{2}\,\dd\theta,\qquad
  B_2=\int\cos\theta\sin\theta\Bigl[\frac{1}{3s}+\frac23\log(1+s)-\frac13-\frac23\log2\Bigr]\dd\theta,
\]
with $r=\sec\theta$ on $[0,\pi/4]$ and $r=\csc\theta$ on $[\pi/4,\pi/2]$, the two halves equal by symmetry.
For $B_2$ the substitution $v=\cos^2\theta$ makes the remaining integral elementary. For $B_1$ the integral
$2\int_0^{\pi/4}\log\bigl(\tfrac12(1+\sqrt{1+\sec^2\theta})\bigr)\dd\theta$ evaluates to Catalan's constant
$G$, $\pi\log2$, and the imaginary parts of $\mathrm{Li}_2(2^{-1/2}\ee^{\ii\pi/12})$ and
$\mathrm{Li}_2(2^{-1/2}\ee^{7\ii\pi/12})$. Kummer's formula writes $\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Li}_2(r\ee^{\ii\theta})$
in Clausen functions $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Li}_2(\ee^{\ii\theta})$ through the auxiliary
angle $\omega=\arctan\bigl(r\sin\theta/(1-r\cos\theta)\bigr)$ \citep{Lewin1981}, and at both points $\omega=\pi/6$.
The logarithms then cancel, and with $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)=G$ and the duplication formula
$\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\theta)=2\mathrm{Cl}_2(\theta)-2\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi-\theta)$ at $\theta=\pi/6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B12closed}
  B_1=\tfrac54\,\mathrm{Cl}_2\bigl(\tfrac\pi3\bigr)-G=0.35271\,14138\ldots,\qquad
  B_2=\tfrac13\log\frac{(1+\sqrt2)^2}{2(1+\sqrt3)}=0.02151\,58182\ldots.
\end{equation}
Every corner moment is therefore elementary apart from the two constants $G$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$. All
of them are universal numbers, computed once and stored.

\paragraph{A well-conditioned evaluation.} The corner moments are monomial moments about the singular corner,
and contracting them with the weights expanded in powers about that corner is ill-conditioned in the way the
power moments of a homogeneous medium are \citep{NestorCoupling2026}: at degree two it loses about a digit
(Table~\ref{tab:bases}). The blocks are therefore evaluated in a well-conditioned basis, and the corner
moments serve as an independent check. On a box away from the singular point the weights are written,
exactly, in Legendre polynomials of the box's own coordinates and contracted with the Legendre moments of
the kernel, which a tensor Gauss rule gives. A box anchored at the singular point is split into three Duffy
pyramids about it \citep{Duffy1982}. In pyramid $k$, with $t_k=\varrho$ and $t_j=\varrho v_j$ for the other two
axes in units of the box's sides, every kernel $\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_j$ is $\varrho^{d}$ times its value on
the far face, $d$ its degree of homogeneity, and each one-dimensional weight is $(A_i\varrho w_i)^{m_i}r_i(A_i\varrho
w_i)$, its zero of order $m_i$ at the corner factored out exactly and $r_i$ written in Legendre polynomials.
The radial integrand is then $\varrho^{2+d+\sum m_i}$ times a polynomial, integrated exactly, and the face
integrand is smooth, the far face lying at a distance of two half-widths from the singular point. Two details
decide the last digit. The Gauss weights are taken from a $30$-digit table, since those of standard library
routines carry relative errors growing with the order, of $4\times10^{-15}$ at $32$ points and up to
$7\times10^{-13}$ at $40$; and the tensor-product sums are carried out one axis at a time, since a single
running sum over the $32^3$ nodes of a box loses about $2\times10^{-15}$.

\section{Cells on opposite sides of the interface}\label{sec:transmission}

For a source cell in A and a field cell in B the static coupling is the transmitted field, the whole of it,
with no direct part. Its potentials are those of Eq.~\eqref{eq:potentials} with $\zeta=z-z'=|z|+|z'|$, and the
singular point is where the two cells touch at the interface (Figure~\ref{fig:interface}b). The $275$
canonical terms are integrable at that point, as the image's are. In the separation form the vertical
weight is now a cross-correlation in $v-v'$, like the lateral ones, and the corner moments are those of
Section~\ref{sec:blocks}.

The other arrangements follow by symmetry. A field cell in A with a source in B, and two cells in B, are
the problems above reflected in the plane $z=0$ with the two media exchanged. Under the reflection the
displacement $u_z$ and the shear strains $\gamma_{zx}$, $\gamma_{zy}$ change sign, so the block is conjugated
by $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1,1,1,1,-1,-1)$ in the order $(u_z,u_x,u_y,\varepsilon_{zz},\varepsilon_{xx},
\varepsilon_{yy},\gamma_{xy},\gamma_{zx},\gamma_{zy})$, and each cell polynomial is multiplied by $(-1)$ to
its power of $\xi_z$.

\section{The next term: the order \texorpdfstring{$\omega^2$}{omega squared}}\label{sec:omega2}

The static image removes the part of the reflected integrand that does not decay. What remains decays as the
square of the frequency over the wavenumber, and its leading term can be removed the same way. Let the
reflected displacement tensor of Section~\ref{sec:static}, in the frame of $\bk$, be expanded at fixed
$(q,z,z')$ in powers of the frequency,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omegaexpansion}
  \mathbf G^{R}(q;z,z';\omega)=\mathbf G^{R}_0+\omega^2\,\mathbf G^{R}_1+O(\omega^4),
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf G^{R}_0$ the static image. With $\mathbf u=\mathbf u_0+\omega^2\mathbf u_1+\dots$ the Navier
equations $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma(\mathbf u)+\rho\,\omega^2\mathbf u+\mathbf F\delta=\mathbf 0$ give, at
order $\omega^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega2navier}
  \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma(\mathbf u_1)=-\rho\,\mathbf u_0,
\end{equation}
with no source: $\mathbf u_1$ and its traction are continuous at $z=z'$ and at $z=0$. In each region the
static field $\mathbf u_0$ is a polynomial times $\ee^{\pm qz}$, the exponential being a double root of the
static operator, so a particular solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega2navier} is a polynomial of degree two higher
times the same exponential. To it are added the static solutions $(a+bz)\ee^{\pm qz}$ regular in the region.
The whole-space problem is solved first; its $\omega^2$ term, that of Kelvin's field, then forces the
interface conditions that fix the reflected term $\mathbf G^{R}_1$ and the transmitted term $\mathbf G^{T}_1$.
Every entry of $\mathbf G^{R}_1$ is $\ee^{q(z+z')}$ times a polynomial in $z$ and $z'$, of degree at most two
in each and three in all, with coefficients rational in $q$ and in the Lam\'e constants and densities of the
two media. The point propagator follows from $\mathbf G^{R}_1$ by the derivatives of
Section~\ref{sec:static}, and its vertical moments over a cell are elementary.

Three checks confirm the result. When the media coincide, $\mathbf G^{R}_1=\mathbf 0$ and
$\mathbf G^{T}_1$ is the $\omega^2$ term of Kelvin's field. Against the exact kernels, evaluated in $50$-digit
arithmetic, the residual $\mathbf G-\mathbf G_0-\omega^2\mathbf G_1$ falls sixteenfold each time $\omega$ is
halved, as an $O(\omega^4)$ remainder must. And the derivation done independently in two computer-algebra
systems, one with symbolic and one with rational material constants, agrees to $5\times10^{-51}$.

In space the term needs more of the ladder. The divisions by $q$ that turn a rotated entry into a lateral
derivative (Section~\ref{sec:static}) take the lowest power of $q$ in $\mathbf G^{R}_1$ to
$q^{-5}\ee^{-q\zeta}$, whose transform, like that of $q^{-4}\ee^{-q\zeta}$, exists only as a finite part: the
potentials $\Phi_3$ and $\Phi_4$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fppotentials}. Since the finite part of a convergent integral
is the integral, and the finite part is linear,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fpsplit}
  \bK^R=\bK^R_0+\omega^2\bK^R_1+\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\,\mathrm{FP}\!\int_{\mathbb R^2}
  \bigl(\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_0-\omega^2\mathbf Y_1\bigr)\dd^2k,\qquad
  \bK^R_1=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\,\mathrm{FP}\!\int_{\mathbb R^2}\mathbf Y_1\,\dd^2k,
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf Y$, $\mathbf Y_0$, $\mathbf Y_1$ the integrands of the block built from the dynamic kernel,
$\mathbf G^R_0$ and $\mathbf G^R_1$, holds exactly, with no cutoff, when every finite part is taken by the
same continuation in the power of $q$.

\paragraph{The $\omega^2$ block in closed form.} Collected as in Section~\ref{sec:static}, the $9\times9$
$\omega^2$ term of the reflected field is a sum of $744$ terms $c\,z^Az'^B\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_j$ in $76$
families, and that of the transmitted field of $582$ terms in $61$ families, with $j$ up to $4$ and the
coefficients rational in the Lam\'e constants and densities of both media. In each, $81$ terms in $16$ families
carry fewer lateral derivatives than their index $j$: $\Phi_1$ with none, $\Phi_2$ with at most one,
$\Phi_3$ with at most two and $\Phi_4$ with at most three. For these the derivatives do not annihilate the
solid harmonic of the residue, and the finite parts of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fppotentials} are needed as they stand.
Their kernels are of degree $d=j-1-|\balpha|\ge0$, bounded at the singular point, but they are homogeneous
only up to a logarithm. Under $\mathbf x\to\lambda\mathbf x$ the potential becomes
$\Phi_j(\lambda\mathbf x)=\lambda^{j-1}\bigl[\Phi_j(\mathbf x)+\log\lambda\;g_0(\mathbf x)\bigr]$, with
$g_0=-(-1)^nR^nP_n(\zeta/R)/(2\pi\,n!)$ minus the residue, so that every kernel $f=\partial^{\balpha}\Phi_j$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loghomogeneous}
  f(\lambda\mathbf x)=\lambda^{d}\bigl[f(\mathbf x)+\log\lambda\;g(\mathbf x)\bigr],\qquad
  \mathbf x\cdot\nabla f=d\,f+g,\qquad g=\partial^{\balpha}g_0,
\end{equation}
$g$ a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$. It vanishes whenever the lateral derivatives annihilate the solid
harmonic: for every term of the static field, and for $8$ of the $16$ finite-part families. Two of the steps
of Section~\ref{sec:blocks} change. Euler's identity on a box anchored at the singular point acquires a
polynomial moment,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eulerlog}
  \bigl(3+p+q+r+d\bigr)\int_{\rm box}\rho_x^p\rho_y^q\zeta^r f
  =\sum_{\text{far faces}}\bigl|A_i\bigr|A_i^{n_i}\int_{\rm face}\rho^{\mathbf n\setminus i}f
  -\int_{\rm box}\rho_x^p\rho_y^q\zeta^r g,
\end{equation}
and, since $d\ge0$, its factor never vanishes. In a Duffy pyramid the kernel is
$\varrho^d[f+\log\varrho\;g]$ on the far face, so the radial integrand gains a polynomial times
$\varrho^e\log\varrho$, which is integrated exactly from the polynomial's coefficients in shifted Legendre
polynomials and $\int_0^1\varrho^m\log\varrho\,\dd\varrho=-1/(m+1)^2$. Finally, the scaling of a block with the
cell size gains a term: in units of $h$ a family contributes $h^{6+d+A+B}\bigl[\int f+\log h\int g\bigr]$. A
finite part depends on the unit of length, here the kilometre, and the plane-wave remainder of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fpsplit}, whose finite part at small $q$ is taken in the same unit, depends on it in the opposite
way, so that their sum does not. The line of images of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lineofimages}, with its divergence at
$t\to\infty$ dropped, is a different convention: it differs from Eq.~\eqref{eq:fppotentials} by polynomials,
$-\gamma/2\pi$ and $\gamma\zeta/2\pi$ for $j=1,2$ and, for $j=3,4$, quadratics and cubics that are not even
harmonic, so its closed forms are used only where the lateral derivatives annihilate the difference.

The closed form is checked against Eq.~\eqref{eq:fpsplit} itself. The radial integrand of
$\mathrm{FP}\!\int\mathbf Y_1$, the azimuthal integral done, behaves as $q^{-2}$ times an entire function at
small $q$; on $[0,q_s]$ that function is sampled at Chebyshev nodes and each mode is integrated against
$q^{-2}$ exactly, the $q^{-1}$ term giving $\log q_s$, and above $q_s$ Gauss rules are used. For two reflected
pairs whose images do not touch, over every field function and source monomial, the closed-form $\bK^R_1$ and
the finite part of its plane-wave integral agree to $5.1\times10^{-14}$ and $6.0\times10^{-14}$, and for two
transmitted pairs two half-widths apart across the interface the transmitted $\omega^2$ block agrees to
$5.4\times10^{-14}$ and $3.6\times10^{-14}$; moving $q_s$ from $h^{-1}/4$ to $h^{-1}/2$ changes the latter by
about $10^{-13}$. 

\paragraph{The algebraic tail of touching cells.} When both cells touch the interface the integrand of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fpsplit} decays only algebraically in $q$, and a plane-wave reference that truncates it is limited
by its tail (Table~\ref{tab:subtraction}). The tail is integrated analytically instead. With $c=qh$ the vertical
moments of a cell are combinations of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verticalsplit}
  E_k(c)=\int_{-1}^{1}v^k\ee^{c(v-1)}\,\dd v=P_k(1/c)+\ee^{-2c}Q_k(1/c),\qquad
  P_k=\frac1c-\frac kcP_{k-1},\quad Q_k=-\frac{(-1)^k}{c}-\frac kcQ_{k-1},
\end{equation}
with $P_0=1/c$, $Q_0=-1/c$: polynomials in $1/c$, the first from the face of the cell nearest the interface and
the second from the far face. A cell on the interface multiplies them by a prefactor of order one, any other
cell by $\ee^{-2qh}$ or less. The integrand is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:yalg}
  \mathbf Y=\mathbf Y_{\rm alg}+O(\ee^{-2qh}),\qquad
  \mathbf Y_{\rm alg}(\bk)=\sum_{p}\sum_{|n|\le4}q^{p}\,\ee^{\ii n\phi}\,\mathbf H_{p,n}\;
  L_a(\bk)\,L_c(\bk)\,\ee^{\ii\bk\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta},
\end{equation}
the product of the receiver's and the source's $P$ parts with the frame kernel, rotated: finitely many powers
($p$ from $-8$ to $1$ for the blocks here), the nine harmonics of the rotation, and the exact coefficients
$\mathbf H_{p,n}$. Each term is integrated over the whole plane in closed form. The transform of a power and a
harmonic is the Weber--Schafheitlin integral,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weberschafheitlin}
  \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\,\mathrm{FP}\!\int_{\mathbb R^2}q^{p}\ee^{\ii n\phi}\ee^{\ii\bk\cdot\brho}\,\dd^2k
  =\frac{\ii^{n}\ee^{\ii n\theta}}{2\pi}\,
  \frac{2^{p+1}\,\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{|n|+p+2}{2}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{|n|-p}{2}\bigr)}\,\rho^{-p-2}
\end{equation}
(with a factor $(-1)^{|n|}$ for $n<0$), continued in $p$: the convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fppotentials}. The
lateral moments and the phase turn it into an integral over the lateral separation $\mathbf u=\boldsymbol\sigma
+\boldsymbol\Delta$ against the exact one-dimensional weights $w(\sigma)=\int P_E(v)(v-\sigma)^F\dd v$, on the
pieces $[\Delta-2,\Delta]$ and $[\Delta,\Delta+2]$ of each axis. On a piece with the origin at a corner, in polar
coordinates about it, each monomial of the weights times the kernel is $\rho^{d}A(t)$, so that the radial
integral is elementary and the angular one is a Gauss rule on a smooth integrand. Writing $p+\varepsilon$ for
the power, the Gamma ratio, the radial integral $\int_0^{R}\rho^{d+1-\varepsilon}\dd\rho$ and the scaling
$h^{-\varepsilon}$ are each expanded in $\varepsilon$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laurentconst}
  \mathrm{FP}=\bigl[\varepsilon^0\bigr]\;\Gamma\text{-ratio}(\varepsilon)\times
  \int_{\rm piece}\!(\cdots)\,\rho^{-p-2-\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)\times h^{-\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
the constant term of the product. At most one factor has a pole: the Gamma ratio when the kernel is a
polynomial in $\brho$, and the radial integral when $d+2=0$; the pole times the next order of the other factor
gives the logarithms, and a zero of the Gamma ratio against a radial pole gives the local, delta-like terms. The
remainder $\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_{\rm alg}$ is integrated by Gauss rules and is below rounding beyond $qh=24$.

The finite part at small $q$ needs the same care. The part of $\mathbf Y_{\rm alg}$ below a split point $q_s$,
which is subtracted from the whole-plane integral, cannot be sampled: it and the whole-plane integral are each
about twenty times the block and cancel, and the powers down to $q^{-7}$ amplify the rounding of any sampled
representation, which left $3\times10^{-10}$. It is taken in closed form as well, from the series of the Bessel
function,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:headseries}
  \mathrm{FP}\!\int_0^{q_s}q^{p+1}J_{|n|}(q\rho)\,\dd q
  =\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-1)^k(\rho/2)^{|n|+2k}}{k!\,(|n|+k)!}\,
  \begin{cases}\dfrac{q_s^{\,e+1}}{e+1},&e\ne-1,\\[4pt] \log q_s,&e=-1,\end{cases}
  \qquad e=p+1+|n|+2k,
\end{equation}
(the factor $(-1)^{|n|}$ for $n<0$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:weberschafheitlin}), whose terms
$\rho^{|n|+2k}\ee^{\ii n\theta}$ are polynomials in $\mathbf u$ and are integrated against the weights

The two corner routes, Duffy pyramids and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eulerlog}, agree to $9\times10^{-16}$ on the blocks of touching cells, and for identical media
$\bK^R_1$ vanishes. Until the closed form was available the term was used in the wavenumber domain, and that
route is kept as the independent reference.
With $\mathbf Y(\bk;\omega)$ the integrand of the reflected block, so
that $\bK^{R}=(4\pi^2)^{-1}\int\mathbf Y\,\dd^2k$, and $\mathbf Y_0$, $\mathbf Y_1$ the integrands built in the
same way from $\mathbf G^{R}_0$ and $\mathbf G^{R}_1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega2split}
  \bK^{R}=\bK^{R}_0+\omega^2\,\bK^{R}_1(Q_0)
  +\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\bigl[\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_0-\omega^2\,\mathbf Y_1\,\theta(q-Q_0)\bigr]\dd^2k,
  \qquad
  \bK^{R}_1(Q_0)=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{q>Q_0}\mathbf Y_1\,\dd^2k.
\end{equation}
Neither $\bK^{R}_0$ nor $\bK^{R}_1(Q_0)$ depends on the frequency: both are computed once for each
arrangement of cells, the first in closed form (Section~\ref{sec:blocks}) and the second as a plane-wave
integral of a closed-form integrand, which needs no reflection coefficients. Only the last integral is
evaluated at each frequency, and beyond $Q_0$ its integrand falls by two more powers of $q$ than with the
static image alone.

Table~\ref{tab:omega2} shows the effect for the block of Table~\ref{tab:subtraction}, at $\omega h/\beta=1/6$
and at $\omega h/\beta=1$, the frequency at which a shear wavelength spans about three cells. The remainder
now converges as $Q^{-6}$. Truncated at $Qh=10$ it leaves $3.6\times10^{-10}$ of the block at the lower
frequency, where the static subtraction alone leaves $1.3\times10^{-6}$ and needs $Qh\approx160$ to do as well;
at $\omega h/\beta=1$ it leaves $3\times10^{-7}$ at $Qh=10$ and $6\times10^{-11}$ at $Qh=40$, where the static
subtraction alone leaves $3\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-7}$.
The transmitted coupling behaves in the same way. For a source cell touching the interface and the field
cell its mirror image in B, so that the two touch across the interface, the shells of
$\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_0$ fall with local orders $3.90$ to $4.17$ and those of
$\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_0-\omega^2\mathbf Y_1$ (with $\mathbf Y_1$ built from $\mathbf G^{T}_1$) with orders $5.86$
to $6.24$; truncated at $Qh=10$ the remainder leaves $2.2\times10^{-10}$ of the block at $\omega h/\beta=1/6$, and
truncated at $Qh=40$ it leaves $4.3\times10^{-11}$ at $\omega h/\beta=1$.

The lower cutoff also settles the precision of the mode sums. Below $Qh=10$ the cancellation between the P and
SV terms (Section~\ref{sec:planewaves}) is mild: evaluated in double precision, the integral over $qh<10$
differs from its $40$-digit value by $7\times10^{-12}$ of the block at $\omega h/\beta=1/6$ and by
$5\times10^{-13}$ at $\omega h/\beta=1$, and the shell $10<qh<20$ by $6\times10^{-11}$ and $2\times10^{-14}$. With
the $\omega^2$ term subtracted, a double-precision plane-wave integral therefore gives the reflected block of a
touching cell to about $4\times10^{-10}$ with the cutoff at $Qh=10$ when $\omega h/\beta=1/6$, and to about
$10^{-10}$ with the cutoff at $Qh=40$ when $\omega h/\beta=1$; with the static image alone the same accuracy
needs cutoffs at which double precision fails. The frequency-independent integral $\bK^{R}_1(Q_0)$ is the slowly convergent one, with an integrand that falls as
$\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_0$ does. Its integrand needs no mode sum: written in the frame of $\bk$ it is
$\ee^{q(z+z')}$ times a polynomial in $z$ and $z'$ whose coefficients are Laurent polynomials in $q$, and in
double precision it agrees with the $40$-digit kernel to $10^{-12}$, an error that enters multiplied by
$\omega^2$. It is not integrated to infinity: by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fpsplit},
$\bK^{R}_1(Q_0)=\bK^{R}_1-(4\pi^2)^{-1}\mathrm{FP}\!\int_{q<Q_0}\mathbf Y_1\,\dd^2k$, the closed-form block less
the finite part below $Q_0$, so that no tail is left. (Integrated by octaves to $Q_1h=160$ with the tail taken from
the $q^{-4}$ law of the last octave, as before the closed form existed, it carried $5\times10^{-7}$ of itself;
by octaves to $Q_1h=640$, $8\times10^{-10}$.) The angular dependence costs little: the rotation of the frame kernel to the direction of
$\bk$ contains only the harmonics $\ee^{\ii n\phi}$ with $|n|\le4$, so at each $q$ the azimuthal rule only sums
the cells' lateral moments against nine harmonics.

Assembled in this way, with the plane-wave integral below $Q_0$ in double precision, the split reproduces the reference values to the size of the remainder it drops. For
identical media the transmitted block must be the touching block of the homogeneous medium, which
\citet{NestorCoupling2026} obtains in closed form with no plane waves; over every field function and source
monomial ($4\times10\times9\times9$ entries), for cells touching across the interface face to face, along an
edge and at a corner, and with the source above or below, the two agree to $0.8$ to $3.2\times10^{-10}$ at
$\omega h/\beta=1/6$ with $Q_0h=10$ and to $1.7\times10^{-12}$ to $5.4\times10^{-11}$ at $\omega h/\beta=1$ with
$Q_0h=40$. For different media the reflected block of a cell with its own image and the transmitted block of
two cells touching across the interface agree with the $40$-digit integrals of Tables~\ref{tab:subtraction}
and~\ref{tab:omega2} to $3.6\times10^{-10}$ and $2.2\times10^{-10}$ at the lower frequency and to
$5.9\times10^{-11}$ and $3.9\times10^{-11}$ at the higher, the remainders of Table~\ref{tab:omega2} and of
the transmitted sweep.

The integral below $Q_0$, the only part evaluated at each frequency, is cheapest in the same frame. Its
integrand is the dynamic frame kernel, rotated, times the cells' lateral moments and phase; the rotation holds
nine harmonics, so the integral is a sum over radial nodes of the harmonics of the frame kernel, one $6\times6$
interface solve per node with the cells' vertical moments in closed form, against the azimuthal integrals
$S_n(q)$ of the lateral moments and phase. These depend on neither the frequency, the media nor the depths of
the cells: they are tabulated once for each lateral offset, as piecewise Chebyshev series, and interpolated
to the radial rule of each frequency, whose panel ends follow the branch points $q=\omega/c$. The result agrees
with the mode sum evaluated at every point of the polar grid to $3\times10^{-15}$ to $2\times10^{-11}$ (the
latter the rounding of the reflected mode sums, which both routes share) and costs a twentieth to a fiftieth
of its time.

\paragraph{The next order.} The perturbation of the static problem continues: at order $\omega^4$,
$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma(\mathbf u_2)=-\rho\,\mathbf u_1$ with no source, and $\mathbf u_2$ is a polynomial
two degrees higher than $\mathbf u_1$ times the same exponential, plus the regular homogeneous solutions; Kelvin's
term and the reflected and transmitted terms $\mathbf G^{R}_2$, $\mathbf G^{T}_2$ follow from the conditions at
$z'$ and at the interface as before. Against the exact kernels at $50$ digits the residual
$\mathbf G-\mathbf G_0-\omega^2\mathbf G_1-\omega^4\mathbf G_2$ falls $64$-fold each time $\omega$ is halved, and for
identical media $\mathbf G^{R}_2=\mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf G^{T}_2$ is Kelvin's $\omega^4$ term. Derived with the Lam\'e
constants and densities as symbols, each entry is the exponential times a polynomial in $q$, $z$, $z'$ and the
media over $q^5$ and a product of $\mu_A$, $\mu_B$, $\lambda_A+2\mu_A$, $\lambda_B+2\mu_B$, $\mu_A+\mu_B$ and the two
factors coupling the media, $\lambda_A(\mu_A+\mu_B)+\mu_A^2+3\mu_A\mu_B$ and $\lambda_B(\mu_A+\mu_B)+3\mu_A\mu_B+\mu_B^2$; at
rational media it agrees identically with an independent derivation in a second computer-algebra system. Its lowest power of
$q$ in the frame is $q^{-5}$, and collected as in Section~\ref{sec:static} the $9\times9$ term is a sum of $1245$
(reflected) and $1002$ (transmitted) canonical terms with potentials up to $\Phi_6$, about $550$ of them finite
parts, each coefficient a polynomial in $z$, $z'$ with coefficients rational in the media. Over the common
denominator the collection is polynomial arithmetic on the numerators alone, and the factors of the denominator
are cancelled by exact division at the end. Their closed-form block $\bK_2$ is formed as $\bK_1$ is, and the split becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega4split}
  \bK=\mathbf D(\omega;Q_0)+\mathbf A_0+\omega^2\mathbf A_1+\omega^4\mathbf A_2+\mathbf E,\qquad
  \mathbf A_n=\bK_n-\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\,\mathrm{FP}\!\int_{q<Q_0}\mathbf Y_n\,\dd^2k,
\end{equation}
$\mathbf D$ the plane-wave integral of $\mathbf Y$ below $Q_0$ at each frequency and
$\mathbf E=(4\pi^2)^{-1}\int_{q>Q_0}(\mathbf Y-\mathbf Y_0-\omega^2\mathbf Y_1-\omega^4\mathbf Y_2)\dd^2k$ the dropped
remainder, which now falls as $Q_0^{-8}$. Each $\mathbf A_n$ is the part of the closed-form block beyond $Q_0$,
obtained without integrating any tail. For $\mathbf A_2$ the subtraction cancels about seven digits, since the
part of $\bK_2$ beyond $Q_0$ is about $10^{-7}$ of it: the finite part below $Q_0$ must be as accurate as the closed
form.

\paragraph{The finite part at small $q$ in closed form.} Near $q=0$ the integrands $\mathbf Y_n$ behave as
$q^{-2}$ ($n=0,1$) and $q^{-4}$ ($n=2$) times entire functions. A finite part taken from samples of the entire
function, at Chebyshev nodes with each mode integrated exactly, is limited by the rounding of the samples, which
the factor $q_s^{1-N}$ amplifies: for $q^{-4}$ it has a narrow window, $6\times10^{-9}$ of $\bK_2$ with $24$ nodes
on $[0,3/h]$, $4\times10^{-11}$ with $40$ and $5\times10^{-10}$ with $48$. It is taken instead in closed form, with
every factor of the integrand an exact power series in $q$: the frame kernel's Laurent polynomial; the vertical
moments,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verticalseries}
  \int_{\rm cell}f(v)\,z^{a}\ee^{sqz}\,\dd z=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(sq)^n}{n!}\int_{\rm cell}f(v)\,z^{a+n}\,\dd z,
\end{equation}
exact polynomial moments; and the lateral moments with the phase, for each harmonic, by the series of the Bessel
function against the exact lateral weights, Eq.~\eqref{eq:headseries}. Their product is a Laurent series
$\sum_pq^p\,\mathbf R_{p,n}$ for each harmonic, and each term's finite part on $[0,q_s]$ is a power of $q_s$, or
$\log q_s$ at the power $-1$. The series converge for every $q$, but lose digits as $q_s$ times the largest distance
$R$ within the pair grows, so $q_s=\min(1/h,4/R)$; above $q_s$ Gauss rules are used. At order zero, whose
integrand is integrable at $q=0$, the closed form agrees with plain integration to $1.6\times10^{-15}$ and
$1.4\times10^{-14}$; and $\bK_2$ less the closed-form finite part below $Q_0$ agrees with the integral of
$\mathbf Y_2$ beyond $Q_0$ by octaves (whose shells fall as $q^{-6}$) to $1.9\times10^{-15}$ (reflected) and
$1.2\times10^{-15}$ (transmitted) of $\bK_2$. With it, Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega4split} reproduces the $40$-digit
references to the size of $\mathbf E$ (Table~\ref{tab:omega4}): a factor of about $2^8$ per doubling of $Q_0$, and
below $10^{-12}$ at $Q_0h=40$ when $\omega h/\beta=1$.

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verification}

Each step was checked against a route that shares no step with it; Table~\ref{tab:verification} collects the
results. The media are A with $\alpha=5.0$, $\beta=3.0$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\rho=2.5$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$ and B with
$\alpha=6.5$, $\beta=3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $\rho=2.8$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$, and $h=0.05$\,km. Errors are relative to
the largest entry of the quantity compared, and the corner moments are measured on the scale $2^{p+q+r}$ of
each monomial.

The checks are of four kinds. The spectral solutions were derived independently in two computer-algebra
systems, and their spatial forms were compared with direct two-dimensional Fourier integration of the
spectral kernels. The corner moments were compared with three routes: Euler's reduction with Gauss rules on
the far faces, tanh-sinh quadrature of the singular volume integral itself, and an evaluation at thirty
digits that integrates one coordinate exactly and then the other two with Duffy coordinates at the corner
\citep{Duffy1982}. We note that adaptive three-dimensional cubature applied directly with the singular point
at a corner, or over the semi-infinite column, can report convergence while in error by as much as
$5\times10^{-7}$, which is why the references integrate one coordinate exactly. The blocks of cells away from
the singular point were compared with a tensor Gauss rule over both cells of the canonical kernel, which
shares neither the separation form nor the exact weights. Two limits close the chain: for identical media
the transmitted blocks must be the touching blocks of the homogeneous medium \citep{NestorCoupling2026},
computed by an unrelated method, and the image must vanish; and the touching block of a cell with its own
image must be the limit of the plane-wave integral \eqref{eq:reflected} of the static spectrum as the
cutoff grows.

The accuracy of the touching blocks themselves is measured against a reference that removes the rounding
of the double-precision routes: the power-basis route carried out in $80$-bit extended arithmetic (unit
round-off $1.1\times10^{-19}$), with the corner moments in closed form at $40$ digits, Gauss rules refined to
extended precision, and the kernels and exact weights rounded to extended precision only. Its own rounding,
bounded by the unit round-off times the sum of the magnitudes of its terms, is below $6\times10^{-17}$ of the
blocks. Against it (Table~\ref{tab:bases}) the well-conditioned evaluation is at round-off for both field
degrees, $3\times10^{-16}$ to $1.2\times10^{-15}$, and the power basis is the same at degree one but loses up to
a digit at degree two. The fully independent check of these blocks, the plane-wave integral, is limited to
$2.3\times10^{-8}$ by its own convergence. With identical media the image vanishes identically, to the last
bit, on both sides of the interface.

The plane-wave route, which shares no step with the closed forms, has to reach every pair of cells near the
interface, not only the touching ones. Two devices make it do so; both belong to the reference, not to the
method. First, the cutoff $\theta(q-Q_0)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega2split} makes the frequency-independent
corrections $\bK^{R}_0-(4\pi^2)^{-1}\int_{q<Q_0}\mathbf Y_0$ and $\bK^{R}_1(Q_0)$ ring in space, so that they
decay with the cells' lateral offset $d$ only as about $d^{-3/2}$: for a row of cells touching the interface at
$\omega h/\beta=1/6$ and $Q_0h=10$ the first is still $1.4\times10^{-5}$ of the touching block at $d=32h$. A
smooth window $\chi(q)$, zero below $Q_0/4$, one above $Q_0$, and $\ee^{-1/t}/(\ee^{-1/t}+\ee^{-1/(1-t)})$ on the
ramp between ($t$ the position on the ramp), moves the ramp integrals from the plane-wave part to the
corrections,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:windowsplit}
  \bK^{R}=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_{q<Q_0}\bigl[\mathbf Y-\chi(\mathbf Y_0+\omega^2\mathbf Y_1)\bigr]\dd^2k
  +\Bigl(\bK^{R}_0-\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int(1-\chi)\,\mathbf Y_0\,\dd^2k\Bigr)
  +\frac{\omega^2}{4\pi^2}\int\chi\,\mathbf Y_1\,\dd^2k+\mathbf E,
\end{equation}
with the same remainder $\mathbf E$ beyond $Q_0$; the corrections then decay faster than any power of $d$,
to $1.2\times10^{-6}$, $5.9\times10^{-9}$, $9.1\times10^{-11}$ and $4.7\times10^{-12}$ of the touching block at
$d=8h$, $16h$, $24h$ and $32h$, reflected and transmitted alike (Table~\ref{tab:locality}). The window costs
nothing: the corrections are the sharp ones plus integrals of $\chi\mathbf Y_0$ and $\chi\mathbf Y_1$ over the
ramp, below $Q_0$, which the plane-wave part gives back, and the windowed and sharp blocks agree to the
rounding of the plane-wave part. The wider the ramp, the faster the decay: a ramp from $Q_0/2$ gives $3.2\times10^{-11}$ at $32h$
instead of $4.7\times10^{-12}$, and a polynomial step, smooth only to finite order, decays algebraically and
stalls near $10^{-10}$. The $\omega^2$ correction decays alike and is two to three orders smaller at this
frequency: $2.7\times10^{-11}$ of the touching block at $16h$. So the corrections are local: beyond $12h$,
$16h$ and $24h$ (six, eight and twelve cells) they fall below $10^{-6}$, $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-10}$ of the
touching block, and the block is the windowed plane-wave integral below $Q_0$ alone. Within that radius they depend
only on the two depths and the lateral offset, so a row or grid of cells along the interface needs them once
per pair of depths and offset, and the costly $\omega^2$ part only within its own, smaller radius.

Second, the azimuthal integrals of the cells'
lateral moments $L_aL_c$ against the phase $\ee^{\ii\bk\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta}$ of a lateral offset
$\boldsymbol\Delta$ and the nine harmonics of the rotation need no rule that grows with $q|\boldsymbol\Delta|$:
by the Jacobi--Anger expansion of the phase,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobianger}
  S_n(q;\boldsymbol\Delta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_p\ii^pJ_p(q|\boldsymbol\Delta|)\,\ee^{-\ii p\phi_\Delta}\,
  c_{-n-p}(q),
\end{equation}
where $c_m(q)$ are the azimuthal Fourier coefficients of $L_aL_c$. These are band-limited by the cell, not by
the offset ($|m|\le13$, $51$ and $148$ above $10^{-12}$ at $qh=1$, $10$ and $40$), so they are computed once for
every offset.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The static image carries the whole singularity of the coupling at a welded interface, and in closed form it
costs no more than the touching blocks of a homogeneous medium: a fixed set of universal corner moments,
contracted with exact rational weights and with coefficients rational in the Lam\'e constants. The dynamic
remainder is smooth, and the plane waves of Section~\ref{sec:planewaves} carry it at a cost that does not
depend on how close the cells are to the interface, since the subtraction has removed what made the
integral converge slowly.

The split also preserves what makes a frequency sweep cheap in a homogeneous medium, where the closed-form
blocks are set up once and each frequency costs little. Here the static blocks $\bK^{R}_0$ and the
coefficients $\bK^{R}_1(Q_0)$ of the $\omega^2$ term are computed once for each arrangement of cells, and at
each frequency only the remainder is integrated, over $qh<Q_0h$, with one interface solve per radial node
and the angular integrals of the cells' lateral moments tabulated once, so that a frequency costs tens of
milliseconds per block at $\omega h/\beta=1/6$ and a few hundred at $\omega h/\beta=1$. With the $\omega^4$ term,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega4split}, also in closed form for any media, the dropped remainder falls as $Q_0^{-8}$, so a
lower cutoff, and fewer radial nodes per frequency, reach the same accuracy; higher orders follow the same way,
each a polynomial two degrees higher, but the gain is then in the cost of the remainder, not in its accuracy.

For a stack of layers the same construction applies interface by interface. The singular part of the
reflected and transmitted couplings comes only from the interface that the cells touch, and is the static
image of that interface; reflections from other interfaces reach a cell after travelling at least one layer
thickness and are smooth. The plane-wave representation extends to the stack through the reflection and
transmission matrices of the layered medium, composed by the recursions of the reflectivity method
\citep{FuchsMuller1971,Kennett1983}. The coupling of every cell with every other is then available from the
up- and down-going factorisation alone, without tabulating the stratified Green's tensor, which is the
storage advantage that motivates the approach; a two-way march in depth that applies the volume operator in
this way is the subject of further work.

One limitation remains: the closed form of the base integrals is stated for cubic corner boxes, $a=b=c$,
which is all a grid of cubes requires; for unequal sides the angular integrals are evaluated by quadrature.
